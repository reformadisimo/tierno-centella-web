Sexual harassment (acoso sexual) and sex-based harassment (acoso por razón de sexo) are forms of discrimination, and the law treats them especially severely. Your employer has a duty to prevent them, to have a protocol to investigate them and to protect you when you report them. If it does not, it is liable too.

> If you feel you are in danger, call 016 (the helpline for victims of violence against women, free of charge and it leaves no trace on your phone bill) or 112.

## What each one is

The Equality Act (Ley Orgánica 3/2007, Article 7) distinguishes two types of conduct:

- **Sexual harassment (acoso sexual)**: any verbal or physical behaviour of a sexual nature that violates your dignity, especially when it creates an intimidating, degrading or offensive environment. Comments about your body, innuendo, messages or images with sexual content, touching, propositions in exchange for improvements at work.
- **Sex-based harassment (acoso por razón de sexo)**: hostile treatment because you are a woman, even if it has no sexual content. Belittling your work "because women aren't cut out for this", sidelining you after you announce a pregnancy, ridiculing you for asking for reduced working hours (reducción de jornada).

It does not have to be repeated over months to be sexual harassment: a single serious act can be enough. And it does not matter whether the person doing it is your boss, a coworker or a client.

## What your employer is obliged to do

- Have a protocol against sexual and sex-based harassment that the staff know about. It is compulsory for all companies, whatever their size.
- Investigate quickly and confidentially when you report it.
- Protect you during the investigation, for example by separating you from the harasser without you being the one who loses out.
- Not retaliate against you for reporting it. If you are dismissed or sanctioned for reporting, the measure is void (nula).

If your employer has no protocol or does not apply it, that can also be reported to the Labour Inspectorate (Inspección de Trabajo). We explain what to demand in [the anti-harassment protocol guide](/en/workplace-harassment/anti-harassment-protocol).

## How it is proved

In discrimination cases the law helps you with the evidence: it is enough for you to provide reasonable **indications (indicios)**, and then it is the employer that has to prove there was no harassment or that it acted correctly. Useful indications are:

- Messages, emails, voice notes or images you received.
- Recordings of conversations you took part in.
- A diary with the date, time, place, what happened and who was there.
- Coworkers who saw it or whom you told at the time.
- Medical or psychological reports linking your distress to work.

## What you can claim

Through the employment route, depending on your situation:

1. **For the harassment to stop and to be compensated** for the harm suffered, with a claim for protection of fundamental rights (tutela de derechos fundamentales) against the employer and, where appropriate, against the harasser.
2. **To leave the company with the compensation for an unfair dismissal (despido improcedente)** (33 days per year), plus damages, by asking the judge to terminate your contract. We explain this in [quitting your job because of harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/quitting-your-job-because-of-harassment).
3. **For your sick leave to be treated as a work accident (accidente de trabajo)**, if the harassment has made you ill. You are paid more and from the first day.

In addition, sexual harassment is a crime (Article 184 of the Criminal Code, Código Penal). The criminal and employment routes can run at the same time, and it is best to decide the order with a lawyer.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it sexual harassment if it was only comments?

It can be. Comments with sexual content about your body or your private life that create an offensive environment fit the legal definition, without any need for physical contact.

### What if the person harassing me is a client?

Your employer also has to protect you from clients, suppliers or users. If it knows and does not act, it is liable.

### Can I be dismissed for reporting it?

No. It would be retaliation and the dismissal would be void (despido nulo), with reinstatement and moral damages.

### How long do I have to claim?

It depends on the route. While the harassment continues, you can claim. If you are dismissed, the deadline is 20 working days. For damages, generally one year. And the criminal route has its own deadlines. It is best to get advice as soon as possible.
