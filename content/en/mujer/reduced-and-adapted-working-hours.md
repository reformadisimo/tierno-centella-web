You have two different tools for balancing work and family life (conciliar). **Reduced working hours (reducción de jornada)** is a right your employer cannot refuse: you work fewer hours and are paid less. **Adapted working hours (adaptación de jornada)** means asking for a different way of working (another schedule, remote work, a continuous working day) without reducing your hours; your employer has to negotiate it with you and can only reject it for objective reasons.

> Always ask for both in writing and keep the stamped copy or the email. If you are later dismissed or sanctioned, that date is your best evidence.

## Reduced working hours

Article 37.6 of the Workers' Statute (Estatuto de los Trabajadores) allows you to reduce your daily working hours:

- If you are responsible for a **son or daughter under 12**, or you care for a relative who cannot look after themselves.
- By **between one eighth and one half** of your working day.
- With a proportional reduction in salary.
- With you choosing the schedule within your usual working day. The collective agreement (convenio) may set criteria, but the general rule is that you decide.
- Giving 15 days' notice, or the period set by your collective agreement, and stating when you start and when you finish.

Your employer cannot refuse the reduction. It can disagree with the schedule you propose if it has serious organisational reasons, and then the judge decides.

## Adapted working hours

Article 34.8 allows you to ask for changes in how you work, without reducing your hours or your pay:

- Changing your schedule or moving to a continuous working day (jornada continua).
- Working remotely some days or every day.
- Distributing your hours differently.

You can ask for it to care for children under 12, and also for your partner, relatives up to the second degree or other dependent people who live with you, explaining the need.

Your employer has to open a negotiation with you for a **maximum of 15 days** and reply to you **in writing**: accepting, proposing an alternative or rejecting the request with objective reasons. When the circumstances end, you can go back to your previous working hours.

## If your employer refuses

You have **20 working days** from when you are notified of the refusal to file a claim (demanda) with the Social Court (Juzgado de lo Social). It is a fast, urgent procedure, with no prior conciliation, and in the same claim you can ask for compensation for the harm the refusal has caused you.

Do not let the deadline pass thinking you will negotiate it later: if it passes, you have to ask again and start from scratch.

## Your job is protected

If you are dismissed for having asked for reduced or adapted working hours, or while you are using them, the dismissal is void (despido nulo) unless the employer proves a real, unrelated cause. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can my employer refuse my request for reduced working hours?

No. It is a right. It can only dispute the specific schedule you propose, and if you cannot agree, the judge decides.

### Can they make me change shifts when I reduce my hours?

In principle, the reduction takes place within your ordinary working day and it is up to you to set the schedule. Unless your collective agreement says otherwise, they cannot impose a different shift on you.

### Can I ask to work remotely to care for my children?

Yes, through adapted working hours. Your employer has to negotiate it and, if it rejects it, explain objective reasons in writing.

### Do I get the same unemployment benefit if I am dismissed while on reduced hours?

To calculate unemployment benefit (prestación por desempleo) and compensation, the contribution bases are counted as if you had worked full time during the reduction for childcare.
