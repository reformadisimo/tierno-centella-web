If you are dismissed while pregnant, on birth leave, on breastfeeding leave (permiso de lactancia) or on reduced working hours (reducción de jornada) for caring, the law presumes the dismissal is void (despido nulo). You do not have to prove that this is why you were dismissed: it is the employer that has to prove a real cause that has nothing to do with it. If it cannot, the judge orders it to reinstate you and to pay you all the salary you did not receive.

> The deadline does not wait: you have 20 working days from the dismissal to file the conciliation request (papeleta de conciliación). Work out your last day with [the deadline calculator](/herramientas/plazos) (in Spanish).

## Who is protected

Article 55.5 of the Workers' Statute (Estatuto de los Trabajadores) automatically protects:

1. Pregnant workers, from the first day of pregnancy, **even if the employer does not know**. The Constitutional Court (Tribunal Constitucional) made this clear in 2008: the protection does not depend on having told them.
2. Anyone on leave for birth, adoption, guardianship or foster care, for risk during pregnancy or breastfeeding, or taking parental leave (permiso parental).
3. Anyone who has requested or is taking breastfeeding leave, a reduction or adaptation of working hours for caring, or a leave of absence (excedencia) to care for children or relatives.
4. Anyone who has returned to work after birth, adoption or foster care leave, during the **12 months following** the birth, adoption or foster placement.

The protection is the same for mothers and fathers. What the law protects is maternity and care, whoever is doing the caring.

## What happens if the dismissal is void

- You return to your job on the same conditions.
- The employer pays your salary from the dismissal until your return.
- If your right not to be discriminated against has been violated, you can also ask for compensation for moral damages.

The employer cannot swap reinstatement for money, as it can with an unfair dismissal (despido improcedente). If, after what you have been through, you do not want to go back, you need to look at how to negotiate your exit, and it is best to do that with a lawyer before the conciliation hearing (acto de conciliación).

## The most common traps

### "You have not passed your probation period"

Since 2019, if the employer ends your probation period (periodo de prueba) while you are pregnant (or on birth leave), that decision is void unless it proves the reason has nothing to do with the pregnancy. Keep the dates: when you told them and when they let you go.

### "Your temporary contract is ending"

Not being renewed is not automatically void, because the protection in Article 55.5 refers to dismissal. But if your contract was in fact fraudulent (for example, you were chaining contracts for a permanent post) or there are indications that you are not being renewed because of the pregnancy, the termination can be declared discriminatory and void. It is worth having it reviewed.

### "It is an objective dismissal for economic reasons"

It may be, but the employer has to prove the cause and that choosing your post has nothing to do with your pregnancy or your leave. If the figures do not add up, or they have only dismissed the person who was on leave, the dismissal may be void.

### "Sign here, it's an agreement"

Do not sign a voluntary resignation or an exit agreement without a lawyer seeing it. If you sign the final settlement (finiquito), add "no conforme" (not in agreement) next to your signature. We explain this in [what signing the finiquito as "no conforme" means](/blog/finiquito-firmar-no-conforme) (in Spanish).

## What to do from today

1. Keep the dismissal letter and note the date you were given it.
2. Gather proof of the key dates: the medical report of the pregnancy, the email in which you told them, your request for reduced working hours or breastfeeding leave.
3. Keep the messages from the previous weeks: changes in how you were treated, comments about the pregnancy, duties taken away from you.
4. Do not let the 20 working days go by.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I be dismissed if the employer did not know I was pregnant?

They can dismiss you, but the dismissal will be void unless the employer proves a justified cause unrelated to the pregnancy. The protection does not depend on whether they knew.

### What if I am dismissed in the year after returning from birth leave?

During the 12 months following the birth you are still protected. If the employer does not prove a real cause, the dismissal is void.

### Does this protection also apply to fathers?

Yes. The protection for birth leave, breastfeeding leave, reduced working hours and leave of absence for caring applies to any parent who exercises them.

### Can I claim moral damages as well as reinstatement?

Yes, if the dismissal violates your right not to be discriminated against. You have to ask for it expressly in the claim (demanda) and justify the amount; judges usually take as a reference the fines in the law on employment offences (ley de infracciones laborales).
