A dismissal during medical sick leave (baja médica) is not void (nulo) just because you are on sick leave. It can be if the real reason is your illness: since July 2022, Law 15/2022 on equal treatment (Ley 15/2022 de igualdad de trato) includes illness among the prohibited grounds of discrimination. If you provide indications that you were dismissed for being ill, it is the company that has to prove it had another reason.

## What changed with Law 15/2022

For years, the Supreme Court (Tribunal Supremo) took the view that dismissing someone for being ill was not discriminatory. Illness was only really protected when it lasted so long that it was treated as equivalent to a disability. In practice, most dismissals during sick leave were declared unfair (improcedentes): the company paid the compensation and that was the end of it.

Law 15/2022 of 12 July, on equal treatment and non-discrimination (integral para la igualdad de trato y la no discriminación), came into force on 14 July 2022 and changed the starting point:

- Its Article 2 includes "illness or health condition" (enfermedad o condición de salud) among the grounds on which nobody may be discriminated against.
- Its Article 26 says that discriminatory acts are null and void (nulos de pleno derecho).
- Its Article 27 presumes that there is moral damage (daño moral) when discrimination is proven, which opens the door to additional compensation.
- Its Article 30 shares the burden of proof: if you provide well-founded indications, the company has to justify its decision objectively and reasonably.

> If the dismissal is void, the company has to take you back and pay you the wages you did not receive since the dismissal. If it is unfair, it can choose to pay you compensation instead of taking you back.

## Void, unfair or fair: what it depends on

The courts agree on one thing: there is no automatic nullity. Being on sick leave when you are dismissed is not enough. What is examined is why you are dismissed.

### When it points to void

The chances of nullity are greater when indications like these appear:

- The dismissal comes just after you report your sick leave or just after you return from it.
- The letter uses generic wording ("poor performance", "loss of trust") without specific facts.
- The company asked about your diagnosis, pressured you to come back or made comments about your periods of sick leave.
- The same has happened to other coworkers on sick leave.

An example: the High Court of Justice of Murcia (Tribunal Superior de Justicia de Murcia), in its ruling 226/2026 of 12 March, declared void the dismissal of a worker whom the company called in to ask about his sick leave for stress and depression, and dismissed that same day. The court held that the real reason was the sick leave and set compensation for moral damage.

### When it stays unfair or fair

Conversely, the High Court of Justice of Madrid, in its ruling 976/2025, rejected nullity in the case of a female worker dismissed a week after reporting three days of rest. It held that closeness in time is an indication, but that the company countered it with objective data about her performance.

If the company proves that the reason is real and has nothing to do with your health, the dismissal can be fair (procedente). If the reason is not proven but there are no indications of discrimination either, it is usually unfair.

> The High Courts do not always decide these cases in the same way. That is why every detail counts and it is advisable to prepare the evidence well from the start.

## If your illness is long or leaves lasting effects

When the illness causes lasting limitations that prevent you from working on equal terms, it can be treated as a disability for discrimination purposes, even if you do not have a certificate. In that case, the company also has an obligation to try reasonable adjustments (ajustes razonables) to your post.

The Supreme Court, in its ruling 1152/2025 of 27 November, confirmed the nullity of a dismissal for unfitness (ineptitud) of a worker with lasting effects from a work accident, because the company did not try to adapt his post. The fact that the INSS (Instituto Nacional de la Seguridad Social) had not granted him permanent disability did not prevent that conclusion.

## What to do if you are dismissed while on sick leave

1. **Write down the dates.** When your sick leave started, when you reported it and when you were dismissed.
2. **Keep everything.** Messages, emails or calls in which the company asked about your health or pressured you.
3. **Do not give more medical information than necessary.** The company has no right to know your diagnosis.
4. **Sign the letter only as received** and the final settlement (finiquito) as "not in agreement" (no conforme).
5. **Claim within the deadline.** You have 20 working days (días hábiles) from the dismissal. Work out your date with the [deadlines tool](/en/tools/deadlines).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can they dismiss me while I am on sick leave?

Yes, sick leave does not prevent dismissal. What they cannot do is dismiss you for being ill. If the real reason is your illness, the dismissal can be void.

### Do I have to prove that I was dismissed because of my sick leave?

Not entirely. You have to provide reasonable indications. From there, it is the company that has to prove that the dismissal had a real reason unrelated to your health.

### What do I gain if the dismissal is declared void?

Reinstatement in your post, the wages you did not receive since the dismissal and, normally, compensation for moral damage. The amount depends on the case.

### Do I keep receiving sick pay after the dismissal?

Yes. If you are on sick leave when the contract ends, you keep receiving temporary incapacity benefit (incapacidad temporal) for as long as the sick leave lasts. If the sick leave is for a common illness, the amount becomes that of unemployment benefit (paro) and that time is deducted from the unemployment benefit you are entitled to afterwards.
