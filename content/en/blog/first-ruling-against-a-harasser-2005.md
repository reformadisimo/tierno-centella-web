On 30 September 2005, Social Court No. 29 of Madrid (Juzgado de lo Social n.º 29 de Madrid) ordered a company and the director who was harassing one of its employees, David del Olmo, to pay him 30,000 euros jointly and severally (de forma solidaria). According to the firm, it was the first ruling in Spain to order the harasser personally to pay in this way, together with the company. The High Court of Justice of Madrid (Tribunal Superior de Justicia de Madrid) later dismissed the appeal. The employee's lawyer was Jesús Tierno.

## What David went through

What was judged was not a bad day or a one-off argument. It was sustained humiliating treatment by his director. According to the facts of the case, David suffered:

- Shouting.
- Mockery in which his gestures were imitated.
- Humiliating remarks.

And all with one aim: to make him leave the company.

It is the pattern we now easily recognise as workplace harassment (acoso laboral) or mobbing: behaviours that, one by one, someone might try to play down, but that together are aimed at wearing a person down until they leave.

## What the court decided

The court did not stop at finding in the employee's favour against the company. It also convicted the director, as the person responsible for the harassment, and it did so jointly and severally.

### What joint and several liability means

Joint and several liability (condena solidaria) means that the employee can claim the full 30,000 euros from either of the two: the company or the director. Each is liable for the whole amount, and they then settle between themselves who pays what.

For the victim this has two important consequences:

1. **It guarantees payment.** If one of those ordered to pay does not pay, the victim can go after the other.
2. **It names the person responsible.** The ruling does not stop at saying that "the company" failed. It also says who was doing the harassing.

The ruling was appealed to the High Court of Justice of Madrid, which dismissed the appeal. The judgment was upheld.

## Why it was pioneering

When people talk about harassment at work, they usually think of the company's liability, since it is the company that has the duty to protect its staff. But the harm is caused, directly, by a specific person.

A court ordering the company and the harasser to pay jointly and severally changed the message: whoever harasses is also liable, with their own assets, to the person they have harmed.

> According to the firm, this was the first ruling in Spain that ordered the company and the harasser, jointly and severally, to compensate the victim.

## What has changed since then

Today the law expressly provides for this possibility. Article 177.4 of the Social Jurisdiction Act (Ley Reguladora de la Jurisdicción Social) of 2011 states that the victim of harassment can direct their claims both against the employer and against any other person who is responsible.

In addition, since 2010 serious workplace harassment has been a crime. Article 173.1 of the Criminal Code (Código Penal) punishes with six months to two years in prison anyone who, in an employment or civil service relationship and taking advantage of their position of superiority, repeatedly carries out hostile or humiliating acts against another person that amount to serious harassment.

And in 2023 the International Labour Organization (ILO) Convention 190 on violence and harassment at work came into force in Spain, which requires harassment to be prevented and victims to be given ways to obtain redress.

## What this story can tell you if you are going through something similar

Every case is different and no ruling guarantees the outcome of another. But David's case leaves some useful ideas:

- **What is happening to you has a name.** Shouting, mockery and humiliation aimed at making you leave can be harassment.
- **It can be proved.** In a harassment trial, witnesses, messages, emails, medical reports and other evidence are assessed. Start keeping them now. The [evidence map](/en/tools/harassment-evidence-map) will help you.
- **It is not only the company that is liable.** The person who harasses can also be convicted.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I sue my boss directly, and not only the company?

Yes. The law allows the victim of harassment to claim against both the company and the person who harasses. If you want that person to be ordered to pay, you have to sue them in the same proceedings.

### What does it mean for the judgment to be joint and several?

That you can claim the full amount from any of those ordered to pay. If one does not pay, you can go after the other.

### How much can you receive for workplace harassment?

It depends on each case: on the harm, how long it lasted, the after-effects and the evidence. In this case it was 30,000 euros, but that figure is not a reference for other cases.

### Is there a deadline to claim compensation for harassment?

Yes. As a general rule, a claim for damages in the employment field has a time limit of 1 year. If the harassment ends in dismissal, the deadline to challenge it is 20 working days (días hábiles).
