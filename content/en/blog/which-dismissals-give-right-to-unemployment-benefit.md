Yes. **Every dismissal gives you the right to unemployment benefit (prestación por desempleo, or paro)**, whether it is disciplinary, objective or collective, and whether the judge declares it fair (procedente), unfair (improcedente) or void (nulo). What decides whether you receive unemployment benefit is not the type of dismissal, but that you have lost your job through no choice of your own and that you have paid enough contributions.

> Apply within the 15 working days (días hábiles) following the dismissal so as not to lose days of benefit. Work out how much you are entitled to with [the unemployment benefit calculator](/en/tools/unemployment-benefit-calculator).

## What does give the right to unemployment benefit

- Disciplinary dismissal (despido disciplinario), even if the company says it is fair.
- Objective dismissal (despido objetivo) and collective dismissal (ERE).
- The end of a temporary contract.
- The company ending your probation period.
- The judge terminating your contract because of serious breaches by the company (Article 50). We explain it in [leaving the company with compensation](/en/blog/ending-your-contract-under-article-50).
- Leaving after a transfer or a substantial change in your working conditions that harms you, with the compensation provided for by law.

## What does not give the right to unemployment benefit

- **Voluntary resignation (baja voluntaria)**. That is why you should never resign without getting advice, even if the situation is unbearable: there are ways to leave with compensation and with unemployment benefit.
- Voluntary leave of absence (excedencia voluntaria), while it lasts.
- Not returning to your job after a leave of absence or a period of leave when the company offers it to you.

## The requirements

1. Having paid contributions for at least 360 days in the last six years.
2. Being registered as a jobseeker (demandante de empleo).
3. Not having reached the ordinary retirement age.

If you do not reach 360 days, you may be entitled to an allowance (subsidio).

## What if I challenge the dismissal?

Challenging it does not stop you from receiving unemployment benefit. If the judge later declares the dismissal unfair and the company chooses to reinstate you, or if it is void, the company pays you the back pay for the proceedings (salarios de tramitación) and what you have received is regularised.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which type of dismissal does not give the right to unemployment benefit?

None: every dismissal gives the right to unemployment benefit if you meet the contribution requirements. What does not give the right is leaving of your own accord.

### Will I lose unemployment benefit if I sign the final settlement?

No. Signing the final settlement (finiquito) does not affect unemployment benefit. Sign it as "no conforme" (not in agreement) if you do not agree with the amounts or with the dismissal.

### How long do I have to apply for unemployment benefit?

15 working days from the day after the dismissal. If you apply later you do not lose it, but you lose the days of delay.

### If I am dismissed while on sick leave, do I get unemployment benefit?

You continue to receive sick pay until you are signed off as fit for work and then you move on to unemployment benefit. The time on sick leave since the dismissal is deducted from the length of the unemployment benefit.
