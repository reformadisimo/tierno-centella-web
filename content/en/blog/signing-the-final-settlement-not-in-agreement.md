Signing the final settlement (finiquito) as "not in agreement" (no conforme) means that you receive the money or the document, but you do not accept that everything has been paid and you do not give up your right to make a claim. It is the most prudent way to sign when you are dismissed or when you are not sure the figures are right. You are also entitled to ask for a workers' representative to be present when you sign.

## What the final settlement is

The final settlement is the document in which the company pays off what it owes you when the contract ends. It normally includes:

- The days worked in the last month.
- Holiday accrued and not taken.
- The proportional part of the extra payments (pagas extra) that have not been prorated.
- Other outstanding amounts: bonuses, commissions, overtime.
- Compensation, if the company accepts that it is owed.

The problem is that many final settlements include a sentence at the end along the lines of "with receipt of this amount, the worker declares that they have been paid in full and have nothing further to claim" (saldado y finiquitado). If you just sign that, the company will try to use it against you later.

## What signing "not in agreement" means

Next to your signature, write by hand:

> No conforme. Pendiente de revisión. Fecha: (the real day on which you sign).

That is, "Not in agreement. Pending review. Date". With this you put on record that:

1. You have received the document (and, where applicable, the money).
2. You do not agree that this is the full amount you are owed.
3. You are not giving up your right to challenge the dismissal or to claim the differences.

The company cannot refuse to pay you what it acknowledges in the final settlement because you sign it this way. Nor are you obliged to sign.

## Your right to a representative

Article 49.2 of the Workers' Statute (Estatuto de los Trabajadores) gives you two guarantees:

- The company must give you a draft of the settlement document when it notifies you of the termination or the notice period. That way you can review it in good time.
- You can ask for a legal representative of the workers to be present when you sign. The final settlement must state whether you sign with a representative or have not used that right. If the company prevents you, you can write this on the receipt itself.

If there is no works council (comité) or staff delegates (delegados) in your company, this right cannot be exercised, but you can still sign as not in agreement.

## What happens if you have already signed without writing anything

All is not lost. The courts have given the final settlement different weight depending on the case. In general, they look at exactly what the document said, whether there was a clear intention to agree to end the contract and whether it involved giving up rights that cannot be waived.

What counts against you is if the text expressly says that you accept the dismissal or that you have nothing to claim, and that you made no reservation. So if you just signed it, it is a good idea to have a lawyer review it as soon as possible. Each case depends on what the document said and what happened when you signed it.

## Check these figures before signing

You do not need to be an expert. Check the basics:

1. **Holiday.** How many days you were entitled to this year, how many you have taken and how many they are paying you for.
2. **Extra payments.** If you receive them prorated in your payslip, they should not appear again.
3. **Last month.** That they pay you for all the days worked up to the effective date (fecha de efectos).
4. **Compensation.** If the dismissal is objective (despido objetivo), it should be 20 days per year. If the company accepts that the dismissal is unfair (improcedente), 33 days per year (or 45 days for the time before 12 February 2012). You can check it with the [compensation calculator](/en/tools/compensation-calculator).

## The deadlines that are running

Signing the final settlement does not stop any deadline. Bear two in mind:

- **To challenge the dismissal:** 20 working days (días hábiles) from the day after the dismissal. August counts. It is a strict time limit (plazo de caducidad): if it passes, there is no going back.
- **To claim amounts owed** (for example, differences in the final settlement): 1 year, under Article 59 of the Workers' Statute.

> If you want to challenge the dismissal, do not wait until your doubts about the final settlement are resolved. The 20-working-day deadline runs from the first day.

## Frequently asked questions

### Am I obliged to sign the final settlement?

No. But if you do not sign it, the company may not give you a copy or may delay payment. That is why the usual practice is to sign as not in agreement.

### If I sign as not in agreement, do they still pay me?

Yes. The company must pay you what it acknowledges in the final settlement. The reservation only makes it clear that you can claim more.

### Does signing the final settlement mean accepting the dismissal?

It should not, especially if you sign as not in agreement. Even so, always read the text: if it expressly says that you accept the dismissal, do not sign it without getting advice.

### Can I claim differences in the final settlement months later?

Yes, within the 1-year deadline for claiming amounts owed. The dismissal itself can only be challenged within 20 working days.
