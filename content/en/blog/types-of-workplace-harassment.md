Workplace harassment (acoso laboral) is hostile, repeated treatment aimed at isolating you, humiliating you or making you leave. It can come from above, from your own level or even from below, and it can pursue different goals. Knowing which type it is helps you decide whom to claim against and what evidence to look for.

> Not sure whether what is happening to you is harassment? Take [the workplace harassment test](/en/tools/harassment-test).

## By where it comes from

### Top-down: from a boss or superior

This is the most common. A superior uses their power to harass you: they strip you of your duties, criticise you in public, set you impossible targets. The company is liable, and since the ruling the firm obtained in 2005, the harasser personally can be liable too.

### Horizontal: between coworkers

Coworkers at your own level exclude you, spread rumours or make fun of you. The company is liable if it knows and does not act. We explain it in [harassment by coworkers](/en/workplace-harassment/harassment-by-coworkers).

### Bottom-up: from people who report to you

Less common, but it exists: a group of subordinates sabotages a manager's work to discredit them or bring them down.

### Mixed

It starts at one level and others join in, or management looks the other way while coworkers harass.

## By what it is after

- **Strategic**: forcing you to leave so as not to pay for a dismissal.
- **Managerial or disciplinary**: subduing you or punishing you for not giving in, for making claims or for standing out.
- **Perverse**: with no work-related goal, for the pleasure of dominating or destroying another person.
- **Based on sex or maternity**: the treatment changes after you announce a pregnancy or ask for reduced working hours. We explain it in [Women at work](/en/women-at-work).

## What is not harassment

A one-off conflict, an argument or a reasonable work demand is not harassment. Nor is a bad atmosphere suffered equally by all the staff, although it may be a psychosocial risk that the company has to assess.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it have to last to be harassment?

There is no fixed minimum. Judges assess whether the behaviour is repeated over time and forms a pattern. A very serious isolated act can also have consequences.

### Can I claim against the harasser as well as the company?

Yes. Since 2005 the courts have accepted ordering the company and the person who harasses to pay jointly and severally (de forma solidaria).

### What do I do first?

Start a diary with dates and facts, keep the messages and report it in writing. See [how to report harassment](/en/blog/how-to-report-workplace-harassment).
