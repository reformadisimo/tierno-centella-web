Yes, anxiety or depression can lead to permanent disability (incapacidad permanente), but the diagnosis alone is not enough. The social security office (INSS) and the judges assess whether the disorder is severe, whether it has become chronic after years of development and whether it has not improved enough with treatment. What is decisive is proving what you cannot do in your job because of the illness.

## What permanent disability is

It is the situation of someone who, after treatment, still has serious and foreseeably permanent limitations that reduce or eliminate their ability to work. It has several degrees:

- **Partial (parcial).** You perform less well in your profession, but you can carry on. A single lump sum of 24 months' pay is paid.
- **Total (total).** You cannot do your usual profession, but you can do another. A pension of 55% of the regulatory base (base reguladora), which can rise to 75% from the age of 55 if you are not working.
- **Absolute (absoluta).** You cannot do any work. A pension of 100%.
- **Severe disability (gran incapacidad).** In addition, you need another person's help for basic activities such as eating, dressing or washing. A pension of 100% plus a supplement.

In anxiety and depressive disorders, when it is granted, the most common degrees are total or absolute.

## What is assessed in anxiety and depression

The courts look at several elements at the same time.

### Severity

A serious or severe condition is normally required, such as major depression. Mild or moderate depression, or anxiety that is controlled with treatment, is almost never enough on its own.

### Chronicity

The whole history is examined: years of development, relapses, repeated episodes. The law does not set a number of years. What matters is that the condition has stabilised without sufficient improvement.

### Response to treatment

It is assessed whether you have tried different treatments (medication, psychotherapy, sometimes hospital admissions) without sufficient results. Continuing to take medication does not rule out disability: what is relevant is that the aim is no longer to cure you, but to contain the symptoms.

### Specific limitations

This is the key point. It is not enough to say "I have severe depression". You have to explain what it prevents you from doing: concentrating, dealing with the public, making decisions, coping with pressure, keeping to a schedule or driving, for example.

### Your profession

The same depression weighs more in jobs with a high mental workload, constant dealings with people or responsibility for others. That is why two people with the same diagnosis can get different results.

> The INSS and the judge do not assess the name of the diagnosis, but how it limits you in your daily life and in your particular job.

## Which reports to gather

The medical evidence decides most of these cases. Gather:

1. **Reports from the public mental health service**, from psychiatry and psychology, continuous over time. Follow-up over years is the best evidence of chronicity.
2. **Reports describing the development of your condition**: which treatments you have tried, with what results and what limitations remain. A report that only lists diagnoses falls short.
3. **Reports of hospital admissions, emergency visits or crises**, if there are any.
4. **A private expert report (informe pericial)**, if the case goes to trial. The judge assesses it freely, together with the others.

Ask your psychiatrist to describe specifically what you cannot do and what the prognosis is.

## If the depression comes from work

When anxiety or depression originates in harassment or a workplace conflict, it can be argued that it is a workplace accident (accidente de trabajo) rather than a common illness (enfermedad común). That changes how the pension is calculated and other rights. To argue this, you have to link the disorder to work: emails, written complaints, medical reports, witnesses and reports that rule out other causes.

## If the INSS refuses it

It is common for the first answer to be negative. You have 30 working days (días hábiles) from notification to file a prior administrative claim (reclamación previa). If that is also rejected, you can bring a claim before the Social Court (Juzgado de lo Social). In many mental health cases, the disability is obtained at trial, with good medical evidence.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many years of treatment are needed?

The law does not set a period. What is assessed is whether the condition has stabilised and does not improve enough with treatment. The longer and better documented the follow-up, the better.

### Can it be granted for anxiety without depression?

It is possible, but harder. It has to be a severe and chronic disorder that clearly limits your ability to work. It depends a great deal on the reports and on your profession.

### Can I apply if I am still taking medication?

Yes. Being in treatment does not rule out disability. What is assessed is whether, even with treatment, you still have serious limitations.

### Can the disability be reviewed later?

Yes. The INSS can review the degree if your situation improves or gets worse. While it lasts, it is a good idea to keep up your medical follow-up.
