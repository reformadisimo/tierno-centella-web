The conciliation claim (papeleta de conciliación) is the compulsory first step in challenging a dismissal. It is a document in which you state that you have been dismissed and that you do not agree, and it is filed with the mediation and conciliation service of your autonomous region (comunidad autónoma). In the Community of Madrid this is the SMAC. Filing it on time is what saves your claim.

> It must be filed within the 20 working days (días hábiles) following the dismissal. If the deadline passes, the dismissal can no longer be challenged. [Work out your last day](/en/tools/deadlines).

## What stops the clock

When you file the conciliation claim, the 20-day deadline is **suspended**. It starts to run again on the day after the conciliation hearing (acto de conciliación), or 15 working days after filing if the hearing has not taken place by then. That is why it is not a good idea to leave it until the last day: if you file the claim on day 19, you then have only one day left for the court claim (demanda).

## What it has to say

- Your details and the company's details.
- Your length of service, job category and salary.
- The date of the dismissal and the reason given by the company.
- That you do not agree and what you are asking for: for the dismissal to be declared unfair (improcedente) or void (nulo), with the corresponding compensation or reinstatement.

Anything you leave out of the conciliation claim can be hard to add later. If you suspect the dismissal is void (pregnancy, retaliation, illness), say so from the start.

## The conciliation hearing

A few weeks later, you and the company are called to attend. A conciliation officer (letrado conciliador) tries to get you to reach an agreement. Three things can happen:

1. **Agreement (avenencia)**: the company acknowledges that the dismissal was unfair and pays you an amount. The agreement has the force of a court judgment: if the company does not pay, it is enforced directly.
2. **No agreement (sin avenencia)**: there is no agreement and you file the claim with the Social Court (Juzgado de lo Social) with whatever days of the deadline you have left.
3. **Attempted without effect (intentado sin efecto)**: the company does not turn up. The court route also remains open.

Do not accept a figure at the hearing without first knowing how much you are entitled to. [Calculate your compensation](/en/tools/compensation-calculator) and take it written down.

## Do I need a lawyer for the conciliation?

It is not compulsory, but it is where most agreements are reached, and the difference between one offer and another can be thousands of euros. In addition, the conciliation claim shapes the later court claim.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it compulsory to go to the SMAC in a dismissal case?

Yes. Before suing over a dismissal you have to attempt conciliation, with exceptions such as working for the public administration. If you do not file the conciliation claim, the court will not admit your claim until you do. And if the company does not attend the hearing, nothing happens: it is treated as attempted and you can sue.

### How long does it take for the conciliation to be held?

It depends on how busy the service is. The law provides for it to be held within 15 working days, but sometimes it takes longer. If 15 days pass without it being held, the deadline for the court claim starts to run again.

### Can I file the conciliation claim online?

Yes, in most regions, including Madrid, it can be filed electronically with a digital certificate or an electronic identification system.

### What if I work for the public administration?

If you are a contracted employee (personal laboral) of a public administration, there is no conciliation claim: the court claim is filed directly, within the same 20-working-day deadline.

### Can what I sign at the conciliation be changed later?

No. The conciliation agreement is binding. That is why it is a good idea to know what you are entitled to before you sign.
