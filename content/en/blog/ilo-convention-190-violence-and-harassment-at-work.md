Convention 190 of the International Labour Organization (ILO) (Convenio 190 de la OIT) is the first international treaty on violence and harassment at work. It has been in force in Spain since 25 May 2023. It protects not only employees with a contract, but also trainees, job seekers and people who have been dismissed, and it covers harassment by message or on the way to work.

## What Convention 190 is

The ILO adopted it in Geneva on 21 June 2019. Spain acceded to it on 8 March 2022, it was published in the Official State Gazette (Boletín Oficial del Estado, BOE) on 16 June 2022 and it came into force for Spain on 25 May 2023.

As an international treaty published in the BOE, it forms part of Spanish law. Judges can apply it and use it to interpret our laws.

## How it defines violence and harassment

Its Article 1 defines violence and harassment in the world of work as unacceptable behaviours and practices, or threats of them, whether "a single occurrence or repeated", that aim at, result in or are likely to result in physical, psychological, sexual or economic harm. It includes gender-based violence and harassment.

What matters about this definition:

- **It does not require repetition.** A single serious act can fall within the definition.
- **It does not require the harm to have occurred.** It is enough that the conduct could cause it.
- **It includes economic harm,** not only physical or psychological harm.

> In Spain, for psychological harassment (acoso moral) to be a crime (Article 173.1 of the Criminal Code, Código Penal), the hostile or humiliating acts must indeed be repeated. The Convention's definition is broader and serves above all for prevention and protection at work.

## Who it protects

Article 2 extends protection beyond the worker with a contract. It includes:

- People who work, whatever their contractual status.
- People in training, including interns and apprentices.
- Workers who have been dismissed.
- Volunteers.
- Job seekers and job applicants.
- Individuals exercising the authority, duties or responsibilities of an employer.

It applies to all sectors, public and private.

## Where it applies

Article 3 makes it clear that harassment does not have to happen inside the office. It covers what happens in the course of work, linked with it or arising out of it, for example:

1. In the workplace, including public and private spaces where they are a place of work.
2. In places where you eat, rest, change or use the toilet.
3. On work-related trips, travel, events, dinners or training.
4. In work-related communications, including those by email, messaging apps or social media.
5. In accommodation provided by the company.
6. When commuting between home and work.

This is very relevant today: messages in a team WhatsApp group or emails outside working hours can also form part of a harassment situation.

## What it requires of companies

Article 9 obliges States to require employers to take appropriate measures to prevent violence and harassment. In particular:

- To have a policy on violence and harassment, drawn up in consultation with workers and their representatives.
- To take violence, harassment and psychosocial risks into account in managing health and safety.
- To identify and assess those risks and take measures to control them.
- To inform and train staff about those risks and about their rights.

In Spain, the protocol against sexual and sex-based harassment (protocolo frente al acoso sexual y por razón de sexo) is already compulsory for all companies. Psychological harassment is prevented through the psychosocial risk assessment required by the Occupational Risk Prevention Act (Ley de Prevención de Riesgos Laborales), and in many collective agreements (convenios colectivos) and equality plans (planes de igualdad).

## What it means for you if you are being harassed

The Convention does not create a new procedure for reporting. But it strengthens your position:

- It helps you argue that an isolated act, if serious, also deserves protection.
- It reinforces the idea that the company has to prevent, not just react.
- If the company had no protocol, did not assess psychosocial risks or did nothing when it learned of your complaint, that carries weight when holding it liable.

## Frequently asked questions

### Since when has Convention 190 applied in Spain?

Since 25 May 2023. Spain acceded to it in March 2022 and it was published in the BOE on 16 June 2022.

### Can a single episode be harassment under the Convention?

Yes. The Convention includes conduct that occurs only once. Whether it is considered harassment in your particular case will depend on how serious it is and on the other circumstances.

### Does it protect me if I am an intern or in a selection process?

Yes. The Convention expressly protects people in training, interns, apprentices, volunteers and job applicants.

### Does harassment by WhatsApp or email count?

Yes. The Convention covers work-related communications, including those made by digital means.
