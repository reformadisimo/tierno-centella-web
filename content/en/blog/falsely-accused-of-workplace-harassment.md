If you have been accused of harassment and it is not true, the first thing is not to react in the heat of the moment. You have the right to know what you are accused of, to defend yourself and to an impartial and confidential investigation. And it is worth knowing something we see more and more often at the firm: **sometimes the harasser reports their victim first** in order to present themselves as the injured party.

> At the firm we have taken apart false accusations made by several people against a client who was branded a harasser, when in fact she was the victim of a strategy to force her out.

## Your rights during the investigation

- **To know the facts** you are accused of, in enough detail to defend yourself.
- **To be heard** and to give your version, documents and witnesses.
- **Confidentiality**: the company cannot make the complaint public.
- **Impartiality**: whoever investigates cannot have an interest in the outcome.
- **Presumption of innocence (presunción de inocencia)**: they cannot sanction you merely because of the complaint.

We explain how an anti-harassment protocol should work in [what to demand from the harassment protocol](/en/workplace-harassment/anti-harassment-protocol).

## How to defend yourself

1. **Ask in writing** for the specific facts and dates.
2. **Gather your evidence**: emails, messages, calendars, witnesses who can describe what the relationship was really like.
3. **Put the timeline in order**. If the complaint came just after you made a claim or set a limit, that is an important indication.
4. **Do not talk about the matter** with coworkers or on social media. Anything you say can be used.
5. **Get advice before you give your statement** in the investigation.

## If you are sanctioned or dismissed

You have 20 working days (días hábiles) to challenge the sanction or the dismissal. If the investigation did not respect your rights or the evidence does not support the accusation, the measure can be annulled or declared unfair (improcedente). [Work out your deadline](/en/tools/deadlines).

## If you are the victim

If the false complaint is part of the harassment you are suffering, say so and provide your own evidence. The map of what you need is in [the evidence map](/en/tools/harassment-evidence-map), and the ways to make a claim are in [how to report workplace harassment](/en/blog/how-to-report-workplace-harassment).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can they dismiss me just because I have been reported?

No. A complaint is not evidence. To sanction you, the company has to prove the facts after an investigation with proper safeguards.

### Can I find out who reported me?

It depends on the case. Confidentiality protects the person who makes the complaint, but you have the right to know the specific facts so that you can defend yourself.

### What happens if the complaint is shown to be false?

The company can take measures against whoever made the complaint in bad faith, and in serious cases there may be criminal liability. The protection given to people who report does not cover accusations made knowing they are false.
