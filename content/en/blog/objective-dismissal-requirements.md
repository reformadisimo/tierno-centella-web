For an objective dismissal (despido objetivo) to be valid, the company must give you a letter that explains the cause with specific facts, make available to you at that same moment compensation of 20 days' salary per year worked and give you 15 days' notice. If it does not prove the cause or gets the letter or the compensation wrong, the dismissal may be unfair (improcedente).

## What an objective dismissal is

It is a dismissal based on a cause that, in principle, is not your fault. Article 52 of the Workers' Statute (Estatuto de los Trabajadores) lists these causes:

- **Ineptitude (ineptitud).** That you cannot do your job because of a lack of ability that is known or that arose after you started. One that already existed before the end of the probation period cannot be relied on afterwards.
- **Failure to adapt to technical changes.** Only if the changes are reasonable, the company first offered you a course to adapt and at least two months have passed.
- **Economic, technical, organisational or production causes.** Losses, a fall in sales, changes in the way of working or in demand, when they affect fewer workers than a collective dismissal.
- **Insufficient budget (insuficiencia presupuestaria)** in certain contracts with non-profit organisations that depend on public programmes.

> Dismissal for justified absences, even intermittent ones, no longer exists. It was repealed in February 2020.

## The formal requirements

Article 53 of the Statute requires three things.

### 1. A written letter stating the cause

The letter must explain why you are being dismissed. It is not enough to say "economic causes" or "reorganisation". It must set out facts that allow you to know what you are being blamed for or what has happened in the company, so that you can defend yourself.

If the dismissal is for economic, technical, organisational or production causes, the company must also give a copy of the letter to the workers' representatives, if there are any.

### 2. Compensation of 20 days per year, at the same time as the letter

The compensation is 20 days' salary per year worked, with shorter periods prorated by months, up to a maximum of 12 months' pay. It must be made available to you at the same moment you are handed the letter: in cash, by bank transfer that day or by cheque.

There is only one exception: if the dismissal is for economic causes and the company does not have the liquidity to pay it. In that case, it must say so in the letter, and if you dispute it, it will have to prove it.

### 3. 15 days' notice

Fifteen days must pass between the letter being handed over and the date of the dismissal. During that time you are entitled to 6 hours' leave a week, without losing pay, to look for another job.

If the company does not give you the notice, that does not make the dismissal unfair. What it has to do is pay you the salary for those 15 days.

> Sign the letter only as received, with the real date. From the day after the effective date (fecha de efectos) you have 20 working days (días hábiles) to challenge it.

## When it is unfair

An objective dismissal is declared unfair when:

- The company does not prove the cause it relies on.
- The letter does not explain the cause sufficiently.
- The compensation was not made available to you at the same time as the letter, without the lack-of-liquidity exception.
- The copy was not given to the representatives when it was compulsory.

If it is unfair, the company chooses between taking you back or paying you the unfair dismissal compensation: 33 days per year, up to a maximum of 24 months' pay (and 45 days for the time before 12 February 2012). What it has already paid you as an objective dismissal is deducted. You can compare the two figures in the [compensation calculator](/en/tools/compensation-calculator).

If the company made a mistake in calculating the compensation because of an excusable error, the dismissal does not become unfair: it only has to pay you the difference.

## When it can be void

An objective dismissal is void (nulo), and the company has to take you back, when:

- It is discriminatory or breaches a fundamental right.
- It affects a pregnant worker, someone taking birth or childcare leave, or other situations protected by law, unless the cause is proven.
- It is used to get round the rules on collective dismissal.

A case we see often: dismissal for ineptitude of someone with a long-lasting illness or after-effects. The Supreme Court (Tribunal Supremo), in its judgment 1152/2025 of 27 November, declared it void because the company had not first tried to adapt the worker's job.

## What to do if you are dismissed on objective grounds

1. Check whether they paid you the compensation on the same day as the letter. Keep the proof.
2. Check whether the letter gives specific facts or only general phrases.
3. Write down the date it was handed over and the effective date.
4. Sign the final settlement (finiquito) as "not in agreement" (no conforme).
5. Apply for unemployment benefit (paro) within the following 15 working days.
6. Consult a lawyer before the 20-working-day deadline runs out.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does accepting the objective dismissal compensation stop me from challenging it?

No. You can accept it and still challenge the dismissal. If the dismissal is declared unfair, you are paid the difference up to 33 days per year.

### Can they dismiss me for being on sick leave many times?

Objective dismissal for justified absences no longer exists. If they dismiss you because of your sick leave, it may be a discriminatory dismissal on grounds of illness.

### What happens if they did not give me the 15 days' notice?

The dismissal is still valid on that point, but the company has to pay you the salary for those 15 days.

### Am I entitled to unemployment benefit after an objective dismissal?

Yes. An objective dismissal is a legal situation of unemployment (situación legal de desempleo). Apply within the following 15 working days.
