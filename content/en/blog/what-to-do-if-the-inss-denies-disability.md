If the social security office (INSS) denies your permanent disability (incapacidad permanente) or recognises a lower degree than you believe you are entitled to, you have 30 working days (días hábiles) from notification to file a prior administrative claim (reclamación previa). If it rejects it or does not reply within 45 days, you can sue before the Social Court (Juzgado de lo Social). A refusal is not the end: many disabilities are recognised at trial.

## Read the decision carefully

First of all, look at exactly what the decision (resolución) says:

- **Whether it denies the disability** because it considers that your injuries are not serious enough.
- **Whether it recognises a lower degree**: for example, total disability (incapacidad total) when you had asked for absolute disability (incapacidad absoluta).
- **Whether it denies it on administrative grounds**, such as not having the required contribution period.

Together with the decision there is usually the opinion of the disability assessment team (the EVI, Equipo de Valoración de Incapacidades, which many people call the medical tribunal or tribunal médico) and the report of the assessing doctor. Ask for a copy of your complete file if you do not have it: there you will see which injuries were taken into account and which were not.

Write down the date on which you received the notification. That is the date that counts for the deadlines.

## Step 1: the prior administrative claim

This is a written document addressed to the INSS itself in which you ask it to review its decision. It is compulsory: without it you cannot go to court.

> You have 30 working days from notification. Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays do not count. If the deadline passes, you lose the chance to challenge that decision.

In the claim it is advisable to:

1. Explain why you do not agree.
2. Point out the injuries or limitations that the INSS has not assessed or has assessed wrongly.
3. Attach new or more complete medical reports, if you have them.
4. State the degree you are asking for.

Anything you do not raise here may be harder to introduce later, so this is an important step.

## Step 2: the INSS's reply

The INSS has 45 days to reply. Two things can happen:

- **It replies in writing.** If it agrees with you, the problem is over. If it rejects the claim, the period for suing opens.
- **It does not reply.** After 45 days, the claim is deemed rejected by administrative silence (silencio). There is no need to wait any longer.

## Step 3: the court claim before the Social Court

You have 30 days to file the court claim (demanda) from when you are notified of the rejection or from when it is deemed rejected by silence. These 30 days are working days for court purposes: Saturdays, Sundays, public holidays, the month of August and the days between 24 December and 6 January do not count.

> You can check your dates with the [deadlines tool](/en/tools/deadlines). If in doubt, do not cut it fine: file earlier.

At trial, the judge assesses all the medical evidence: the reports from your doctors, the INSS's opinion and, if you provide one, an expert report (informe pericial). The judge is not obliged to follow the INSS's opinion.

### Prepare the evidence well

Most of these trials are decided by the medical evidence. Some key points:

- **Reports that describe limitations**, not just diagnoses. What you cannot do in your job and why.
- **The link with your profession.** Total disability is assessed in relation to your usual profession (profesión habitual), so you need to explain what tasks it requires and which ones you can no longer do.
- **A medical expert**, if the case justifies it. They can explain to the judge what the reports do not make clear.
- **Submit on time.** Since April 2025, documents and expert reports must be filed with the court at least ten days before the trial.

## What happens to your sick leave in the meantime

If you were on sick leave (baja) when the disability was denied, your situation depends on what the decision says and whether you have been signed off as fit for work (alta). In general:

- If you have not been signed off, the sick leave can continue up to its maximum duration.
- If after the refusal you again need sick leave for the same condition within the following 180 days, it has to be authorised by the INSS.
- If you are signed off, in principle you have to return to your job, even if you are challenging the decision.

This is a delicate moment. If you cannot go back to work, find out what options you have before you stop going.

## Other routes

If your health has got much worse since the decision was issued, a new application with updated reports may be considered. And if you already have a recognised degree, you can ask for a review on the grounds that your condition has worsened (revisión por agravamiento).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I go straight to court without filing a claim with the INSS?

No. The prior administrative claim is compulsory. Without it, the court will not admit the court claim.

### What happens if I miss the 30 working days?

You lose the chance to challenge that decision. The only option left would be to file a new application if your situation has changed.

### Do I need a lawyer for the trial?

At the Social Court it is not compulsory at first instance, but it is highly advisable: the medical evidence and the deadlines involve many technical details.

### How long does the trial take?

It depends on the court and its workload. It usually takes months. In the meantime, keep up your medical follow-up: recent reports help.
