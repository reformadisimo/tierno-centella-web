In October 2026, compensation for unfair dismissal (despido improcedente) is still 33 days' salary per year worked, up to a maximum of 24 months' pay. If your contract started before 12 February 2012, the time before that date is paid at 45 days per year. Europe has criticised this system, but the law has not changed.

## The general rule: 33 days per year

A dismissal is unfair when the company does not prove the cause it relies on or does not meet the formal requirements. In that case, Article 56 of the Workers' Statute (Estatuto de los Trabajadores) gives the company two options:

- **Take you back** in your job, paying you the wages you did not receive since the dismissal (known as back pay, or salarios de tramitación).
- **Pay you compensation**, and the contract is treated as ended.

The company has 5 days from the judgment to choose. If it says nothing, it is understood to be taking you back.

The compensation is 33 days' salary for each year worked. Periods of less than a year are prorated by months. The maximum is 24 months' pay.

> If you are a workers' representative, the choice between reinstatement and compensation is yours, not the company's.

## If you started working before 2012

The 2012 labour reform reduced compensation from 45 to 33 days per year. So as not to disadvantage those who already had a contract, a mixed system remains:

1. For the time worked up to 11 February 2012, you receive 45 days per year.
2. For the time worked from 12 February 2012, you receive 33 days per year.
3. The total cannot exceed 720 days' salary.
4. Exception: if the period before 2012 on its own already exceeds 720 days, that figure applies as the maximum, with an absolute cap of 42 months' pay.

## How it is calculated, step by step

To make an estimate you need three figures: your length-of-service start date (fecha de antigüedad), the date of the dismissal and your gross annual salary (including extra payments).

1. **Daily salary.** Divide your gross annual salary by 365.
2. **Time worked.** Count the years and months. A part of a month counts as a full month.
3. **Days of compensation.** Multiply the years by 33 (or apply the two periods if you started before 2012).
4. **Amount.** Multiply the days by the daily salary.

A simple example: with a salary of €30,000 gross a year, the daily salary is around €82.19. With 6 years worked after 2012, you are entitled to 198 days, around €16,274.

You can do the calculation with your own figures in the [compensation calculator](/en/tools/compensation-calculator). The result is a guide only: the salary that counts may include bonuses, commissions or payments in kind, and that is often disputed.

## Compensation and tax

Dismissal compensation is exempt from personal income tax (IRPF) up to the amount the law sets as compulsory, with a limit of €180,000. For the exemption to apply, the dismissal must be recognised as unfair in conciliation or by a judgment. Anything agreed above the legal minimum is taxed.

## The European debate: what has happened and what has not

The European Committee of Social Rights, the Council of Europe body that monitors compliance with the European Social Charter, has examined the Spanish system:

- In a decision made public in July 2024, following a complaint by the UGT trade union, it found that the caps on Spanish compensation do not always make good the harm or deter the company.
- In June 2025, following another complaint by the CCOO trade union, it confirmed that conclusion and added that judges should be able to consider reinstatement.
- In 2026, the Committee of Ministers of the Council of Europe adopted a resolution asking Spain to change its legislation.

Meanwhile, the Supreme Court (Tribunal Supremo), in its judgment 736/2025 of 16 July, handed down by the full Social Chamber (Pleno de la Sala de lo Social), rejected the idea that judges can award compensation above what the law provides by relying on the European Social Charter. According to the Supreme Court, any change has to come from Parliament.

> As of October 2026 no reform has been approved. The Government has announced that it is working on a change, but until it is published in the Official State Gazette (BOE), compensation remains 33 days per year.

## Unfair is not the same as void

If the dismissal is declared void (nulo) (for example, because of discrimination, as a reprisal or during pregnancy), there is no 33-day compensation: the company has to take you back and pay you the wages you did not receive. In addition, there may be compensation for non-material damage (daño moral). That is why, before thinking about the figure, it is worth knowing what type of dismissal yours is. ## Frequently asked questions

### How much is unfair dismissal compensation in 2026?

33 days' salary per year worked, up to a maximum of 24 months' pay. If you started before 12 February 2012, that time is paid at 45 days per year.

### Has compensation gone up because of Europe's criticism?

No. As of October 2026 the law has not changed and the Supreme Court has said that judges cannot raise it on their own initiative.

### Who decides whether I am taken back or paid?

The company, within 5 days of the judgment. If you are a workers' representative, you decide.

### Which salary is used for the calculation?

The one you were earning at the time of the dismissal, including extra payments and salary supplements. If parts of your salary were undeclared or variable, it may be disputed.
