If you take part in the conversation, recording it without telling the other person is, in general, legal and can be used as evidence of harassment at trial. The Constitutional Court (Tribunal Constitucional) made this clear in 1984: there is no secrecy of communications as against someone who takes part in them. What you cannot do is record other people's conversations, share the recording or record on a massive scale without need.

## Why it is valid if you take part

The Spanish Constitution protects the secrecy of communications (Article 18.3). But that secrecy protects against third parties who intercept what others say, not against the person who is speaking.

The Constitutional Court, in its judgment 114/1984 of 29 November, explained it like this: whoever records a conversation in which they take part does not breach the secrecy of communications, because there is no secrecy as against the person they are talking to.

The Supreme Court (Tribunal Supremo), in its judgment of 20 November 2014 (appeal 3402/2012), admitted as evidence a recording made by a worker when he was handed his dismissal letter. It took into account that the conversation was about work, that the other person was speaking on behalf of the company and that there was already a prior conflict.

> Practical rule: if you are in the conversation and it is about work, you can record it to defend your rights.

## The limits

### Do not record conversations you are not part of

Leaving your phone recording in a room and walking away, or recording what other coworkers say among themselves, can indeed breach the secrecy of communications and privacy. That recording will probably not be valid as evidence and may justify a sanction or a dismissal.

### Do not record private matters

Even if you take part, if the conversation goes into the other person's personal or family life with no connection to work, the recording may conflict with their right to privacy.

### Do not record on a massive scale

A one-off recording to document what is happening to you is not the same as recording everything for months. The High Court of Justice of the Canary Islands (Tribunal Superior de Justicia de Canarias), in its judgment 384/2021 of 2 June, upheld the dismissal of a press officer who had secretly recorded, for months, all his conversations with members of his organisation in order to use them in harassment proceedings. The court did not look at the content: it considered that recording indiscriminately and covertly broke good faith, and the fact that the worker handled confidential information weighed heavily.

### Do not share it

Using it in court is one thing. Sharing it with coworkers, posting it on social media or sending it to third parties is something very different, and may breach the other person's honour or privacy.

### Do not record clients or take data

Recording calls with clients or copying company information to "have evidence" can be very costly. Stick to what concerns you.

## How to use the recording at trial

A valid recording does not win the case on its own. Its weight depends on what can be heard and how it is presented.

1. **Keep the original file** without cutting or editing it. Make backup copies.
2. **Note down the details**: date, place, who is speaking and the context the conversation comes from.
3. **Transcribe the important passages**, indicating the minute and who says what.
4. **Submit it in time.** Since April 2025, in employment trials documentary and expert evidence must be submitted at least ten days in advance.
5. **Back it up with other evidence**: witnesses, messages, emails, medical reports. An isolated recording is challenged more than one that fits with everything else.

> A recording in which you can hear someone shouting at you or humiliating you can be very useful. One in which nothing can be understood, or in which only your version can be heard, adds little.

## Other evidence of harassment

The recording is one more piece of evidence. In harassment cases, the following also tend to carry weight:

- Emails and messages, especially if there is a pattern over time.
- Witnesses, even if they are coworkers who no longer work at the company.
- Complaints you have made in writing to the company or to human resources.
- Medical or psychological reports linking your condition to work.
- A diary with dates and facts, written as things happen.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to say that I am recording?

No, if you take part in the conversation and it is about work. The courts accept these recordings even if the other person does not know.

### Can they dismiss me for recording my boss?

Recording a conversation you take part in to defend your rights is not, in itself, misconduct. Recording others when you are not present, recording on a massive scale or sharing the recordings may be.

### Is a WhatsApp voice message valid?

Voice messages sent to you are messages you have received, and you can submit them as evidence. It is a good idea to keep the phone and the original file.

### Can I record with a hidden camera?

This is more delicate than audio, because images affect privacy and a person's own image more. Consult a lawyer first.
