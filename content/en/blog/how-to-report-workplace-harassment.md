There are five ways to report workplace harassment, and they are not mutually exclusive: an internal complaint to your company, the Labour Inspectorate (Inspección de Trabajo), the Social Court (Juzgado de lo Social), the criminal route and, if you work for a public authority, a claim against that authority itself. The usual approach is to start with a written internal complaint, because it forces the company to act and protects you.

> Before reporting, gather the evidence. See what you have and what you are missing in [the evidence map](/en/tools/harassment-evidence-map).

## 1. The internal complaint, in writing

Write to Human Resources or to the person named in your company's harassment protocol. Describe specific facts, with dates, and ask for the protocol to be activated. Do it by email or with an official entry stamp (registro de entrada) and keep the copy.

From that moment the company **knows** what is happening and is obliged to investigate and protect you. If it does not, it is also liable. And if you are sanctioned or dismissed for reporting, the measure is **void** (nula) as retaliation. We tell you what to demand in [the harassment protocol](/en/workplace-harassment/anti-harassment-protocol).

## 2. The Labour Inspectorate

You can file a complaint, including online. The Inspectorate can visit the company, check whether it has assessed psychosocial risks and whether it has a protocol, and propose penalties. Your identity is not disclosed to the company.

## 3. The Social Court

This is the route to make the harassment stop and get compensation. Depending on your situation:

- **Protection of fundamental rights** (tutela de derechos fundamentales), against the company and, where appropriate, against the person harassing you, so that it stops and you are compensated for the damage.
- **Termination of the contract** at your request (Article 50 of the Workers' Statute, Estatuto de los Trabajadores), with the compensation for unfair dismissal plus damages. We explain it in [quitting your job because of harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/quitting-your-job-because-of-harassment).

## 4. The criminal route

In serious cases, workplace harassment is a crime (Article 173.1 of the Criminal Code, Código Penal). It is reported at a police station or at the court. It can run at the same time as the employment route; it is advisable to decide the order with a lawyer. Read [workplace harassment as a crime](/en/workplace-harassment/workplace-harassment-as-a-crime).

## 5. If you work for a public authority

If you are a civil servant (funcionario) or an interim employee (interino), the claim is against the public authority itself, usually for financial liability (responsabilidad patrimonial), with a one-year deadline. We explain it in [harassment in the public sector and education](/en/workplace-harassment/harassment-in-public-sector-and-education).

## What you should not do

- Resign voluntarily (baja voluntaria): you lose your compensation and your unemployment benefit (paro).
- Confront the person harassing you without witnesses or evidence.
- Wait for it to pass: deadlines run and evidence is lost.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I report harassment after leaving the company?

Yes. You can claim damages within the limitation period, generally one year from the end of the employment relationship or of the situation.

### What if my company has no harassment protocol?

That is a breach you can report to the Labour Inspectorate, and it strengthens your claim for damages.

### Can they dismiss me for reporting?

No. It would be retaliation in breach of the guarantee against retaliation (garantía de indemnidad), and the dismissal would be void.

### Do I have to identify myself in the complaint to the Inspectorate?

Yes, the complaint to the Inspectorate is not anonymous, but your identity is not revealed to the company.
