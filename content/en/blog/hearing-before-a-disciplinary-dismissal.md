Before dismissing you on disciplinary grounds, the company has to give you the chance to defend yourself against what it accuses you of. The Supreme Court (Tribunal Supremo) said so in its ruling 1250/2024 of 18 November 2024, and it applies to dismissals after that date. If the company skips this step without a reason that justifies it, the dismissal can be declared unfair (improcedente).

## What the Supreme Court said in November 2024

For decades, the courts took the view that a company could dismiss you on disciplinary grounds (despido disciplinario) without hearing you first. It was enough to hand you the dismissal letter (carta de despido) setting out the facts and the date.

Ruling 1250/2024 changed that approach. The Supreme Court applied Article 7 of Convention 158 of the International Labour Organization (ILO), which Spain ratified years ago. That article says a worker cannot be dismissed for their conduct or performance without first being given the chance to defend themselves against the charges.

Until then, that article was considered little more than a statement of intent. Since that ruling, it is a real obligation for the company.

> The rule applies to disciplinary dismissals notified after 18 November 2024. Earlier ones are judged under the old rule.

## What exactly the prior hearing is

It is a simple step: before dismissing you, the company tells you what it accuses you of and gives you time to respond. This is the prior hearing (audiencia previa). You can explain your version, deny the facts or provide whatever you have.

The law does not set a specific format. Usually you receive a written notice with the facts and a deadline of a few days to submit your response (alegaciones). Some companies call a meeting.

What matters is that the opportunity is real:

- You have to know what you are accused of in enough detail.
- You have to have a reasonable time to reply.
- Your reply has to arrive before the company decides.

If your collective agreement (convenio colectivo) sets out its own procedure (for example, a disciplinary file with fixed deadlines), the company must follow it.

### When it is not compulsory

Convention 158 itself provides for an exception: when the company cannot reasonably be expected to hear you. Think of a very serious situation that requires acting on the spot. This exception is interpreted case by case and the company has to justify it. It is not a general excuse.

## What happens if the company does not hear you

After the 2024 ruling one question remained: if the hearing is missing, what is the consequence? The Supreme Court answered it in 2025. In its ruling 540/2025 of 4 June, it held that failing to carry out this step makes the dismissal unfair.

Unfair means the company has to choose between taking you back or paying you the legal compensation (33 days' salary per year worked, up to a maximum of 24 months' pay). You can work out an approximate figure with the [compensation calculator](/en/tools/compensation-calculator).

The lack of a hearing does not, on its own, make the dismissal void (nulo). Void dismissal (which forces the company to take you back) is reserved for other cases: discrimination, breach of fundamental rights, retaliation for making a claim, pregnancy and other situations set out in the law. If your dismissal also fits one of these, that is argued separately.

## What to do if you receive a statement of charges

If you receive a written notice accusing you of something and giving you a deadline to respond, that is the prior hearing. Do not let it pass.

1. Read the notice calmly and note the date you receive it.
2. Sign only as received, with the date. Do not sign that you agree with the facts.
3. Gather anything that may help you: emails, messages, rotas, names of witnesses.
4. Reply in writing and within the deadline. Keep a copy showing the date you delivered it.
5. If you can, consult a lawyer before replying. What you say there may be used later at trial.

> Replying does not commit you to anything, but not replying may leave unanswered facts that the company will later use against you.

## And if you have already been dismissed without being heard

If you were dismissed on disciplinary grounds after 18 November 2024 and nobody gave you the chance to explain yourself, that is one more argument to challenge the dismissal. It is not the only one: the court also looks at whether the facts are true, whether they are serious and whether the letter meets the requirements.

Remember that the deadline to make a claim is 20 working days (días hábiles) from the dismissal. Monday to Friday count, excluding public holidays, and August does count. ## Frequently asked questions

### Does the prior hearing also apply to objective dismissal?

No. The ruling refers to disciplinary dismissal, which is based on your conduct or your performance as misconduct. Objective dismissal (despido objetivo) has its own requirements: a letter, compensation and notice.

### How much time do they have to give me to reply?

The law does not set a number of days. It has to be a reasonable period so you can prepare your defence. If your collective agreement sets one, that one applies.

### Can they dismiss me even if my reply is good?

Yes. The hearing gives you the chance to defend yourself, but it does not oblige the company to agree with you. If it dismisses you anyway, you can challenge the dismissal within 20 working days.

### If the hearing is missing, do they have to take me back?

Not necessarily. According to the Supreme Court, the lack of a hearing makes the dismissal unfair, and in that case the company chooses between taking you back or paying you compensation.
