If you have just been dismissed, the most important things in the first 48 hours are not to sign anything as being in agreement, to keep the letter and all the evidence, and to write down the date of the dismissal. From the following day you have 20 working days (días hábiles) to challenge it. You can apply for unemployment benefit (paro) at the same time, because challenging the dismissal does not stop you from receiving it.

## Breathe and write down the date

It is normal to be in shock. Before anything else, write down on paper or on your phone:

- The date on which you were told about the dismissal.
- The effective date (fecha de efectos) shown in the letter (the day the contract ceases to exist).
- Who told you and whether anyone else was present.

The deadline to challenge the dismissal starts on the day after the date of the dismissal: 20 working days. Monday to Friday count, public holidays do not, and August also counts. It is a strict time limit (plazo de caducidad): once it has passed, there is no longer any way to challenge the dismissal.

> 20 working days seems like a lot, but they go quickly. Work out your deadline today with the [deadlines tool](/en/tools/deadlines).

## What to sign and what not

They will put two documents in front of you, sometimes on the same day: the dismissal letter (carta de despido) and the final settlement (finiquito).

### The dismissal letter

You can sign it, but only as received. Write "recibido" (received) and the actual date it is handed to you. Signing to acknowledge receipt does not mean you agree with the dismissal.

If you do not want to sign, the company can hand it to you in front of witnesses. Refusing to sign does not stop the dismissal.

### The final settlement

The final settlement is the payment of what you are owed: days worked in the month, holidays not taken, the proportional part of the extra payments (pagas extra) and, where applicable, the compensation.

If you sign it, add "no conforme" (not in agreement) next to your signature. That way you make it clear that you are receiving the money but not giving up your right to make a claim. You also have the right to ask for a workers' representative to be present when you sign, if there is one in your company.

> Do not sign any document saying that you agree with the dismissal or that you have nothing further to claim without first having it checked by someone you trust.

## Keep everything that could serve as evidence

When you are dismissed, your access to email and your computer is often cut off immediately. Whatever you do not already have is very hard to obtain later.

Gather as soon as possible:

1. The dismissal letter and the final settlement.
2. Your contract and your latest payslips.
3. Emails, messages and documents that you hold legitimately and that relate to what you are being accused of.
4. Appraisals, words of praise or anything that contradicts the reasons in the letter.
5. Names of coworkers who could give evidence.

Do not take client data or confidential company information. That can be used against you.

## Read the letter carefully

The letter has to say why you are being dismissed. Look at three things:

- **The type of dismissal.** Whether it is disciplinary (despido disciplinario: for something the company says you have done wrong) or objective (despido objetivo: for economic or organisational reasons, incompetence or other grounds set out in the law).
- **The facts.** Whether they are specific, with dates, or generic phrases.
- **The compensation.** In an objective dismissal, the company has to make the compensation available to you at the same time as it hands you the letter, with some exceptions.

If the dismissal is disciplinary and took place after 18 November 2024, also check whether the company gave you the opportunity to defend yourself beforehand (the so-called prior hearing, audiencia previa). If it did not, the Supreme Court (Tribunal Supremo) has held that the dismissal is unfair (improcedente).

## Apply for unemployment benefit

You have 15 working days to apply for unemployment benefit (prestación por desempleo). They count from the day after the effective date of the dismissal. If your final settlement pays you for holidays not taken, the period starts to run when those holiday days end.

If you apply late you do not lose the right, but you lose as many days of benefit as days of delay. You need to register as a jobseeker (demandante de empleo) and, to receive it, to have paid contributions for at least 360 days in the previous six years.

Challenging the dismissal does not stop you from applying for unemployment benefit. You can do both at the same time.

## Talk to an employment lawyer

The first step in challenging a dismissal is to file a conciliation claim (papeleta de conciliación) with the mediation service (in Madrid, the SMAC). This stops the clock while the conciliation hearing takes place. If there is no agreement, the claim is filed with the Social Court (Juzgado de lo Social).

A lawyer can tell you whether your dismissal has a chance of being declared unfair or void (nulo), and what you could obtain in each case. Each case depends on its facts and its evidence. ## Frequently asked questions

### From when do the 20 days to challenge it count?

From the day after the effective date of the dismissal. Only working days count: not Saturdays, Sundays or public holidays. August does count.

### If I sign the final settlement, do I lose the right to challenge it?

Signing "no conforme" protects your position. Even so, exactly what the document says matters, so it is a good idea to have a lawyer check it before you sign.

### Can I receive unemployment benefit if I sue the company?

Yes. Challenging the dismissal does not prevent you from applying for unemployment benefit. Apply within the 15 working days.

### What if the letter does not say why I am being dismissed?

A letter without specific facts is one of the reasons why a dismissal can be declared unfair. Keep it and get advice as soon as possible.
