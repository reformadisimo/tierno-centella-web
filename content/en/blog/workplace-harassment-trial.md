If you work for a private company, a workplace harassment trial is held at the Social Court (Juzgado de lo Social), under the procedure for the protection of fundamental rights (tutela de derechos fundamentales), which has priority and is urgent. What counts most in winning it is not how much you have suffered, but that the judge sees a **pattern of repeated conduct**, with dates and a trail, and that the company cannot explain it with objective reasons. Nobody can guarantee the outcome: it depends on the evidence and on the court.

> First of all, look at what evidence you already have with the [harassment evidence map](/en/tools/harassment-evidence-map).

## What the process is like, step by step

1. **The claim.** Your lawyer files the claim for the protection of fundamental rights. In this procedure it is not compulsory to try administrative conciliation first.
2. **The Public Prosecutor (Ministerio Fiscal) is a party.** In proceedings for the protection of fundamental rights it always takes part, in defence of the rule of law.
3. **Conciliation before the court.** On the day of the trial itself, before it begins, an attempt is made to reach an agreement. Many cases end here.
4. **The trial.** It is oral and usually short: the claim is confirmed, the company replies, the evidence is examined (documents, witnesses, experts) and each party sums up.
5. **The judgment.** It arrives in writing, normally weeks later. It can be appealed before the High Court of Justice (Tribunal Superior de Justicia).

If you work for the public administration as a civil servant (funcionario), the route is different. We explain it in [harassment in the public sector and in education](/en/workplace-harassment/harassment-in-public-sector-and-education).

## What tips the balance

- **Repetition and the pattern.** Isolated incidents tend to be interpreted as conflicts. Harassment is a sum: the diary of events, the emails and the changes in your conditions must tell the same story.
- **Well-organised indications.** In this procedure, if you provide serious indications (indicios), it is the company that must justify its decisions (Article 181.2 of the Social Jurisdiction Act, Ley Reguladora de la Jurisdicción Social). We tell you about it in [reporting harassment without evidence](/en/blog/reporting-harassment-without-evidence).
- **What the company did when it found out.** If you reported it in writing and the company did not activate its protocol or investigate, that weighs heavily. See [what to demand from the anti-harassment protocol](/en/workplace-harassment/anti-harassment-protocol).
- **Medical and expert reports.** A report linking your condition to work, and in some cases a psychological expert report, help to prove the harm and to quantify the compensation.
- **Credible witnesses.** Two who saw specific events are better than ten who "heard it said".
- **The consistency of your account.** What you say in the claim, to your doctor and at the trial must match. Exaggerations count against you.

## What can be obtained

It depends on what is asked for and what is proven. Usually the judge declares that your rights were breached, orders the conduct to stop and sets compensation for the damage, including non-material damage. Since 2005, both the company and the person carrying out the harassment can be ordered to pay together. If the harassment makes it impossible to carry on, there is also the option of [leaving with compensation under Article 50](/en/blog/ending-your-contract-under-article-50). We go into detail in [what you can claim if you are being harassed](/en/workplace-harassment/what-you-can-claim-for-harassment).

## Mistakes that lose cases that could have been won

- Letting deadlines pass while waiting to have more evidence.
- Turning up without a diary of events, with everything from memory.
- Breaking off the relationship with an angry letter or on social media.
- Signing a final settlement (finiquito) or an agreement without getting advice.
- Mixing facts that cannot be proven with those that can in the claim: it weakens everything.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does a harassment trial take?

Proceedings for the protection of fundamental rights are urgent and have priority, so they tend to move faster than other employment proceedings. Even so, it depends a great deal on the workload of each court.

### Do I have to give evidence in front of the person harassing me?

They may be in the courtroom as a party. If this affects you particularly, discuss it with your lawyer: there are ways to prepare for it and, in some cases, to ask for measures.

### How much does it cost?

At the Social Court, at first instance, no court fees are paid and, in general, you do not pay the other side's legal costs. The main cost is the lawyer's fees; at our firm we give you a fixed quote in writing before we start.

### Can I keep working while the trial is going on?

Yes. You can also ask for interim measures (medidas cautelares), such as suspending the conduct or a change of post, when the situation justifies it.
