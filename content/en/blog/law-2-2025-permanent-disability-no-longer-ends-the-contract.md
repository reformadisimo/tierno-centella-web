Since 1 May 2025, being granted total or absolute permanent disability (incapacidad permanente total o absoluta) or severe disability (gran incapacidad) no longer ends your contract automatically. This is set out in Law 2/2025 of 29 April (Ley 2/2025). If you want to keep working, you must say so in writing within 10 calendar days (días naturales), and the company must try to adapt your job or give you another compatible one before it can end the contract.

## What happened before

Until April 2025, the Workers' Statute (Estatuto de los Trabajadores) allowed the company to end the contract as soon as the social security office (INSS) granted you total or absolute permanent disability or severe disability. It did not have to pay compensation or find you another job.

That left many people in a difficult situation. For example, someone with total disability for their usual profession could do another job at the same company, but lost their employment anyway.

## What changes with Law 2/2025

The law was published in the Official State Gazette (BOE) on 30 April 2025 and came into force the following day. It amended Article 49 of the Statute and created a new letter n) in its section 1. The central idea: ending the contract is no longer automatic. First, the company has to try to keep you on.

### Step 1: you decide whether you want to stay

You have 10 calendar days from when you are notified of the permanent disability decision to tell the company, in writing, that you want to continue the employment relationship.

> If you do not say so in writing within those 10 calendar days, the company can end the contract. Every day counts, including Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays.

### Step 2: the company has 3 months

From that same notification, the company has a maximum of 3 months to do one of these things:

- **Adapt your job** with reasonable adjustments (ajustes razonables): changes to tasks, working hours, tools or workspace.
- **Move you to another job** that is vacant and available, and that suits your profile and your new situation.
- **End the contract**, but only if it justifies in writing, giving reasons, that none of the above is possible.

It is the company's prevention service (servicio de prevención), after consulting the workers' representatives, that determines which adjustments are possible and which jobs are compatible.

### When the company can end the contract

Only in three cases:

1. When the adaptation would be an excessive burden for the company.
2. When there is no vacant and compatible job.
3. When you are offered a suitable change of job and you turn it down.

## What counts as an excessive burden

To assess this, the cost of the measures is weighed against the size of the company, its resources, its financial situation and its turnover. If there is public funding that covers the cost, the burden is not considered excessive.

In companies with fewer than 25 workers, the law sets a specific threshold: the burden is excessive if the cost of the adaptation, after deducting any funding, exceeds the higher of two amounts: what the unfair dismissal compensation would cost, or six months of your salary.

## And your pension if you keep working?

It depends on the degree and the job:

- **Total disability, same job adapted:** the pension is suspended while you hold that job.
- **Total disability, different job:** the pension may be compatible with your salary if the duties do not coincide with those that gave rise to the disability. This is new ground and there is still little case law on redeployment after the reform.
- **The 20% increase for qualified total disability (total cualificada):** it is not paid while you are working.
- **Absolute or severe disability:** working while registered with social security (con alta) suspends the pension, which resumes when you stop working. The severe disability supplement is not suspended.

Before deciding, it is worth doing the sums. What is worthwhile for you depends on your salary, your pension and your personal situation.

## What to do if you are granted disability

1. **Write down the date** on which you are notified of the decision.
2. **Decide quickly** whether you want to stay and tell the company in writing within the 10 calendar days. Keep a copy showing the date it was delivered.
3. **Ask in writing** to be informed of the adjustments or jobs being considered.
4. **If the company ends the contract**, check whether it really justifies it. If it does not adapt the job when it could, the decision can be challenged, and in some cases the courts have found the lack of reasonable adjustments to be discriminatory.
5. **If the INSS has refused you disability or given you a lower degree**, the deadline for the prior administrative claim (reclamación previa) is 30 working days (días hábiles).

## Frequently asked questions

### Since when does Law 2/2025 apply?

It has been in force since 1 May 2025. If your disability decision is dated close to then, it is worth checking which rules apply to your case.

### Is the company obliged to keep me on?

It is obliged to try: to adapt your job or find you another compatible one. It can only end the contract if it proves that this is not possible or would be an excessive burden, or if you turn down a suitable job.

### What happens if I say nothing within 10 days?

The company can end the contract. That is why it is best to say so in writing as soon as possible.

### Does it affect partial permanent disability?

Partial disability (incapacidad permanente parcial) never ended the contract. The reform concerns total, absolute and severe disability.
