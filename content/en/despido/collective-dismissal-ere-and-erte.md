An ERE (collective dismissal, despido colectivo) is when a company dismisses several workers at once for economic, technical, organisational or production reasons. It ends your contract. An ERTE (temporary layoff scheme) is temporary: it suspends your contract or reduces your working hours for a period of time, and then you go back. In an ERE you are entitled to minimum severance pay of 20 days per year worked, up to a maximum of 12 months' pay, and you can challenge your dismissal within 20 working days.

> If you are given a dismissal letter as part of an ERE, do not wait to see what the unions do. The safe thing is to file your claim within the next 20 working days. Work out your deadline with [the deadline calculator](/en/tools/deadlines).

## When a dismissal is collective

The law (article 51 of the Workers' Statute, Estatuto de los Trabajadores) treats a dismissal as collective when, within a 90-day period, the company dismisses at least:

- 10 workers, if it has fewer than 100.
- 10 % of the workforce, if it has between 100 and 300.
- 30 workers, if it has more than 300.

The complete closure of a company is also a collective dismissal if it affects more than five workers. If the company dismisses people a few at a time to stay below these numbers, those dismissals can be declared void.

## How the procedure works

1. The company opens a consultation period with the workers' representatives. It lasts a maximum of 30 calendar days, or 15 if the company has fewer than 50 workers.
2. During that period they must negotiate in good faith: how many dismissals, which selection criteria and what measures to reduce them.
3. At the end, with or without an agreement, the company tells the labour authority and the representatives what it has decided.
4. It then gives each affected worker their own individual dismissal letter.

If more than 50 people are dismissed, the company must offer an outplacement plan (help finding a new job through an outside firm) lasting at least six months, unless it is in insolvency proceedings (concurso). If you are 55 or over, in many cases the company must pay for a special agreement with Social Security (convenio especial) so you keep paying contributions.

## What you are owed if you are dismissed in an ERE

- Severance pay of at least 20 days' salary per year worked, up to a maximum of 12 months' pay. The agreement can set higher amounts.
- Payment of that severance when you are given the letter.
- Notice, or the salary for those days instead.
- Your final settlement (finiquito): outstanding salary, extra payments (pagas extra) and holiday you have not taken.
- Unemployment benefit, if you meet the contribution requirements.

### When and how to challenge it

There are two routes, and it is important not to mix them up:

- A collective challenge is brought by the workers' representatives against the ERE as a whole.
- An individual challenge is brought by you against your own dismissal, within 20 working days.

You can challenge your dismissal individually even if an agreement was reached in the consultation period. For example, if priority to stay (prioridad de permanencia) was not respected (workers' representatives have it and, depending on the collective agreement, other groups too), if the selection criteria were not applied correctly to you, or if you were chosen for a discriminatory reason. If a collective challenge is under way, your individual claim may be put on hold until it is resolved. To find out how to file your claim, read [the guide on the deadline to challenge a dismissal](/en/dismissal/deadline-to-challenge-a-dismissal).

## What is different in an ERTE

In an ERTE you are not dismissed. The company temporarily suspends your contract or reduces your working hours, for economic, technical, organisational or production reasons, or because of force majeure (fuerza mayor).

What you should know:

- While it lasts, you receive unemployment benefit for the part of your working hours you do not work. The company usually applies to the SEPE (Spanish public employment service) for everyone at once, but check on the SEPE website that your benefit has been granted.
- In ERTEs for economic, technical, organisational or production reasons, the benefit is 70 % of your benefit base (base reguladora) for the first 180 days and 60 % after that, within the current minimum and maximum limits.
- As a general rule, benefit received in this type of ERTE does use up your unemployment entitlement. In some force majeure cases and under the RED Mechanism (Mecanismo RED) the rules are different.
- There is no severance pay, because the contract does not end.
- If the company dismisses you during the ERTE, look carefully at the circumstances: the conditions the company accepted when it received aid or exemptions may affect how the dismissal is classified.

You can also challenge your inclusion in an ERTE individually, for example if the criteria were not applied correctly to you. The deadline is short, so do not let it pass.

## What you should do

- Ask your representatives for the minutes of the agreement and the selection criteria.
- Check that the severance in your letter has been calculated correctly. You can get an estimate with [the severance calculator](/en/tools/compensation-calculator).
- Sign the letter as "received, not in agreement" ("recibí, no conforme").

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I make a claim if the ERE ended with an agreement?

Yes. The agreement does not stop you from challenging your individual dismissal, for example if the selection criteria were not applied correctly to you.

### How much severance am I owed in an ERE?

At least 20 days per year worked, up to a maximum of 12 months' pay. The agreement can improve on this. If the judge rules your dismissal unfair (despido improcedente), it would be 33 days per year.

### Do I get unemployment benefit during an ERTE?

Yes, if you meet the contribution requirements, for the part of your working hours that is suspended or reduced. In most cases that benefit uses up your unemployment entitlement.

### Can I be dismissed after an ERTE?

Yes, but that dismissal is looked at very closely. It is worth checking the commitments the company made and the reason it gives in the letter.
