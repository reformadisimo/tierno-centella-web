In Spain there are three main forms of dismissal: disciplinary dismissal (despido disciplinario), for a serious breach by the worker; objective dismissal (despido objetivo), for reasons relating to the company or the job; and collective dismissal (despido colectivo), when it affects many workers at once. Then, if you challenge it, a judge classifies it as justified (procedente), unfair (improcedente) or void (nulo), and that classification determines your severance pay and whether you can go back to your job. You have 20 working days to make your claim.

> If you have just been dismissed, the clock is already running: 20 working days from the day after the dismissal. You can work it out with [the deadline calculator](/en/tools/deadlines).

## The three forms of dismissal

### Disciplinary dismissal

The company dismisses you because it considers you have committed a serious and culpable breach. The Workers' Statute (Estatuto de los Trabajadores, art. 54) lists, among others, these grounds:

- Repeated and unjustified absences or lateness.
- Indiscipline or disobedience.
- Verbal or physical abuse of the employer, colleagues or their families.
- Breach of contractual good faith or abuse of trust.
- Continuous and deliberate reduction in performance.
- Drunkenness or habitual drug use that affects your work.
- Harassment of colleagues or the employer on grounds of origin, religion, disability, age, sexual orientation or sex, or sexual or sex-based harassment.

Disciplinary dismissal carries no severance pay if it is declared justified. It must be notified in writing, with a letter explaining the facts and the date it takes effect.

Since Supreme Court (Tribunal Supremo) judgment 1250/2024 of 18 November 2024, the company must first give you the opportunity to defend yourself against the accusations (known as a prior hearing, audiencia previa). The Supreme Court has since said that, if it does not do so, the dismissal is unfair.

### Objective dismissal

Here the reason is not your behaviour, but a situation the law considers objective (art. 52 ET):

- Supervening incapacity (ineptitud sobrevenida): you lose an ability needed for the job after you started working.
- Failure to adapt to reasonable technical changes in your job, after the company has offered you training.
- Economic, technical, organisational or production reasons, when they affect fewer workers than required for a collective dismissal.
- In some non-profit organisations, lack of funding for a public programme.

Objective dismissal has its own requirements:

1. A written letter explaining the reason.
2. 15 days' notice (or payment for those days if the company does not give it).
3. Severance pay of 20 days' salary per year worked, up to a maximum of 12 months' pay, which must be made available to you when you are given the letter.

If the company gives economic reasons and cannot pay at that moment, it must say so in the letter. That does not take away your right to severance pay.

### Collective dismissal

This is a dismissal that affects a minimum number of workers within 90 days for economic, technical, organisational or production reasons. It requires negotiation with the workers' representatives. We explain it in detail in the guide on [collective dismissal, ERE and ERTE](/en/dismissal/collective-dismissal-ere-and-erte).

## How a dismissal is classified

When you challenge a dismissal, the judge decides whether it was justified, unfair or void. The company does not choose that label: the judge does.

### Justified (procedente)

The company has proved the reason and followed the correct procedure. In a disciplinary dismissal there is no severance pay. In an objective dismissal you keep the 20 days per year.

### Unfair (improcedente)

The company does not prove the reason or does not meet the formal requirements (a vague letter, no prior hearing in a disciplinary dismissal, notice or severance not made available in an objective dismissal). In that case, the company chooses within 5 days between:

- Taking you back in your job and paying your wages from the dismissal until reinstatement (salarios de tramitación).
- Paying you severance for unfair dismissal: 33 days per year, up to a maximum of 24 months' pay. If you started before 12 February 2012, the time before that date is calculated at 45 days per year.

If you are a workers' representative, the choice is yours, not the company's. You can get an estimate with [the severance calculator](/en/tools/compensation-calculator).

In 2024, the European Committee of Social Rights found that this severance system does not provide sufficient compensation in all cases. For now, the legal amount remains the same.

### Void (nulo)

This is the most serious classification. A dismissal is void when it violates a fundamental right or is based on discrimination, and also in some specially protected cases, such as pregnancy or childcare leave. The consequence is immediate reinstatement with all the wages you did not receive. You can also claim compensation for non-material damage (daños morales). We cover this in [the guide on void dismissal](/en/dismissal/when-is-a-dismissal-void).

## What about unemployment benefit and your final settlement

Any dismissal entitles you to apply for unemployment benefit (prestación por desempleo) if you meet the contribution requirements, even if you decide to challenge it. You do not have to wait for the trial.

The final settlement (finiquito) is the payment of what you are owed: salary for the days worked, the proportional part of extra payments (pagas extra) and holiday not taken. Signing it should not stop you from making a claim, but it is a good idea to write "not in agreement" ("no conforme") next to your signature to make clear that you are only acknowledging receipt.

## Why evidence matters

In a dismissal trial, the company has to prove the facts in the letter. It often tries to do so with emails, cameras, working time records or GPS trackers. If that evidence was obtained without respecting your rights, it may not be valid. In the video for this guide, the firm discusses a ruling on GPS evidence in a dismissal.

You can also provide evidence: messages, witnesses, payslips, performance reviews. Keep everything from day one.

## What you should do now

1. Write down the effective date of the dismissal and keep the letter.
2. Sign the letter and the final settlement as "received, not in agreement" ("recibí, no conforme").
3. Apply for unemployment benefit at the SEPE (Spanish public employment service).
4. File the conciliation claim form (papeleta de conciliación) before the 20 working days are up. We explain how in [the guide on the deadline to challenge a dismissal](/en/dismissal/deadline-to-challenge-a-dismissal).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I be dismissed without a letter?

No. Both disciplinary and objective dismissals must be notified in writing. A verbal or "de facto" dismissal (for example, not letting you in) can still be challenged, and the lack of a letter usually leads to the dismissal being ruled unfair.

### What is the difference between unfair and void dismissal?

In an unfair dismissal, the company can choose to pay you severance instead of taking you back. In a void dismissal, reinstatement is compulsory and you are paid the wages you lost.

### Do I get unemployment benefit if I am dismissed on disciplinary grounds?

Yes. Disciplinary dismissal also places you in a legal situation of unemployment (situación legal de desempleo), as long as you have paid contributions for the minimum time required.

### Is the end of a temporary contract a dismissal?

Not exactly, but if the temporary contract was not justified or contracts were chained together irregularly, the termination can be treated as a dismissal. It depends on each case.
