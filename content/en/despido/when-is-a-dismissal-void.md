A dismissal is void (despido nulo) when it violates a fundamental right (for example, it is retaliation for having made a complaint), when it is based on discrimination or when it affects people the law gives special protection to, such as pregnant workers or people taking leave to care for their children. If the judge declares it void, the company must take you back in your job and pay you the wages you did not receive. It cannot replace this with severance pay.

> Even if you believe your dismissal is void, the deadline is the same: 20 working days to file the conciliation claim form (papeleta de conciliación). Work it out with [the deadline calculator](/herramientas/plazos) (in Spanish).

## What happens when a dismissal is void

A void dismissal has no effect, as if it had never happened. In practice this means:

- Immediate reinstatement on the same terms.
- Payment of your wages from the dismissal until you are reinstated.
- You stay registered with Social Security (alta en la Seguridad Social) during that time.
- The possibility of claiming compensation for non-material damage (daños morales) when a fundamental right has been violated. To set the amount, the courts often use the fines in the LISOS (the law on labour offences and penalties) as a reference.

This is the key difference from an unfair dismissal (despido improcedente), where the company can choose to pay you instead of taking you back. We explain this in [the guide to types of dismissal](/en/dismissal/types-of-dismissal).

## When a dismissal is void

### Discriminatory dismissal or dismissal that violates fundamental rights

The Workers' Statute (Estatuto de los Trabajadores, art. 55.5) declares void any dismissal whose motive is discrimination prohibited by the Constitution or the law, or which violates fundamental rights. Some examples:

- Dismissing you because of your sex, origin, religion, beliefs, sexual orientation, age or disability.
- Dismissing you for joining a trade union or going on strike.
- Dismissing you for reporting harassment, claiming your rights or making a complaint to the Labour Inspectorate (Inspección de Trabajo). This is called protection against retaliation (garantía de indemnidad): the company cannot punish you for standing up for yourself.
- Dismissing you in a way that violates your privacy or your freedom of expression.

An example of protection against retaliation: Labour Court (Juzgado de lo Social) no. 1 of Córdoba declared void the dismissal of a supermarket worker who had reported harassment.

### Pregnancy, birth and childcare

The law automatically protects these people, without you having to prove discrimination:

1. Pregnant workers, from the start of the pregnancy, even if the company does not know about it.
2. People on leave for birth, adoption, guardianship or foster care, risk during pregnancy or breastfeeding, or parental leave.
3. People who have requested or are taking breastfeeding leave, reduced or adapted working hours for care, or leave of absence (excedencia) to care for children or family members.
4. People returning to work after birth, adoption or foster care leave, during the 12 months following the birth, adoption or foster placement.

In these cases, the dismissal only stands if the company proves that it is justified (procedente) for a reason that has nothing to do with the pregnancy or the leave. If it cannot prove this, the dismissal is void even if there was no discriminatory intention.

### Victims of gender-based or sexual violence

The dismissal of victims of gender-based violence or sexual violence for exercising their protection rights, such as reducing or rearranging their working hours, changing workplace or suspending their contract, is void.

### Badly handled collective dismissal and fraud

A collective dismissal (despido colectivo) can be declared void if the company has not negotiated with the workers' representatives, has not provided the required documents or has acted fraudulently or under coercion. Objective dismissals (despidos objetivos) carried out a few at a time to avoid the collective dismissal thresholds are also void.

## What if I am dismissed while on sick leave?

This is one of the most common questions, and the honest answer is: it depends. Being on sick leave (de baja) does not automatically make the dismissal void.

Since Law 15/2022 on equal treatment (Ley 15/2022, de igualdad de trato), illness or health condition is a prohibited ground of discrimination. So if the company dismisses you because you are ill and cannot justify another real reason, the dismissal can be declared void. But the courts have given different answers depending on the case: if the letter is vague or the reason is hard to believe, the chances of the dismissal being declared void increase; if there is a real, proven reason, the dismissal can be justified.

What you should look at is the timeline: when the sick leave started, when you were dismissed, what the letter says and what messages were exchanged in between.

## How to prove a dismissal is void

In cases of discrimination or violation of fundamental rights, the law shares the burden of proof:

1. You have to provide reasonable indications (indicios): that you reported harassment and were dismissed shortly afterwards, that you told the company you were pregnant and then the letter arrived, that the reason given makes no sense.
2. If there are such indications, it is the company that must prove the dismissal had a real reason unrelated to those motives.

Useful indications include emails and messages, dates, witnesses, a recent positive performance review or being treated differently from other colleagues. [The evidence map](/herramientas/mapa-de-pruebas) (in Spanish) gives you a guide to what to gather.

## What you should do

- Keep the letter and all messages from before the dismissal.
- Write down dates: when you told the company about the pregnancy, the sick leave, the complaint or the leave request.
- Do not wait: file the conciliation claim form within the 20 working days.
- If you have been through a situation of harassment, also look at [the workplace harassment section](/en/workplace-harassment), because there may be other claims as well as the dismissal.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the dismissal of a pregnant worker void if the company did not know?

Yes. The protection is objective: the company does not need to have known about the pregnancy. The dismissal only stands if the company proves a justified reason unrelated to it.

### Can I be dismissed for having reported harassment?

No. It would be retaliation contrary to the protection against retaliation (garantía de indemnidad), and the dismissal could be declared void, with reinstatement and compensation for non-material damage.

### If the dismissal is void, can I choose not to go back?

The legal consequence is reinstatement. If going back is not possible because of what you have been through, there are ways to ask for your contract to be terminated with severance pay, but they depend on the case and it is worth assessing them with a lawyer.

### How much is compensation for non-material damage?

There is no fixed figure. The judge sets it according to how serious the case is, and often uses the fines in the LISOS as a reference. You must ask for it expressly in your claim (demanda).
