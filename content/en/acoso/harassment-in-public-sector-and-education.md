If you work in the public sector, the route depends on your type of employment. If you are a civil servant (funcionario) or an interim civil servant (interino), you claim first before the administration itself and then before the administrative courts (jurisdicción contencioso-administrativa), usually through a claim for the administration's financial liability (responsabilidad patrimonial), with a one-year deadline. If you are a contracted public employee (personal laboral), you go to the Social Court (Juzgado de lo Social), just as in a private company.

## First, clarify your situation

People with different types of employment work side by side in the public sector, and that changes which court and which rules apply.

- **Career civil servant (funcionario de carrera).** Your relationship is governed by administrative law. Administrative court route.
- **Interim civil servant (funcionario interino).** Same regime as a civil servant, even though your appointment is temporary. Administrative court route.
- **Contracted staff of the administration (personal laboral).** You have an employment contract, even if your employer is a ministry, a regional department (consejería), a town council or a university. Social court route.
- **Staff at state-funded private schools (centros concertados) or private schools.** You are an employee of a private company. Social court route.

If you are not sure, check your appointment or your contract. Choosing the wrong route can waste time and cost you deadlines.

## Harassment in the public sector looks like harassment in any company

The behaviour is the same: isolation, stripping you of your duties, humiliation, constant criticism, rumours. In the public sector it is also common to use the administration's own tools to put pressure on you: unfounded disciplinary proceedings (expedientes disciplinarios), changes of post, refusal of leave or training, negative reports.

If you want to know whether what is happening to you fits, read [how to know if you are being harassed](/en/workplace-harassment/am-i-being-harassed-at-work).

## Public administration protocols

Public administrations have their own protocols against harassment. The General State Administration (Administración General del Estado) has a specific protocol against workplace harassment, and regional governments, town councils and universities usually have their own. The protocol against sexual and sex-based harassment is also compulsory in the public sector.

Activating the protocol has advantages:

- It records that the administration knows about the problem.
- It forces the administration to investigate and take measures, such as separating the people involved.
- If it does not act, or acts badly, that inaction will be evidence in your claim.

Submit your request in writing and through the official registry (registro), with specific facts. More details in [anti-harassment protocol](/en/workplace-harassment/anti-harassment-protocol).

> Activating the protocol does not stop the deadlines for going to court. Keep an eye on your dates from the start.

## Civil servants and interim staff: the administrative court route

### Financial liability of the administration

The administration is liable for the harm caused by its operation, whether normal or abnormal. If it did not protect you from harassment, or if the harassment came from a superior acting in their official role, you can claim compensation through financial liability (responsabilidad patrimonial).

The usual steps are:

1. Submit a financial liability claim to the administration, explaining the facts, the harm and its link to the public service.
2. Wait for the decision. If it is refused, or there is no answer within the deadline, it is treated as rejected.
3. Go to the Administrative High Court or Administrative Court (Tribunal or Juzgado de lo Contencioso-Administrativo).

> The deadline to claim financial liability is one year. For harm to your health, it runs from when you recover or from when the lasting effects (secuelas) are known. Check your dates in [deadlines](/en/tools/deadlines).

### Protection of fundamental rights

There is also a special procedure in the administrative courts to protect fundamental rights, such as moral integrity. It is faster, but its deadlines are very short. It is worth assessing it with a lawyer as soon as the act you want to challenge happens.

### Evidence

The same evidence works as in the private sector: a diary of events, emails, messages, witnesses, medical reports, and the administration's failure to respond. We explain it in [how to prove harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/how-to-prove-workplace-harassment).

## Contracted staff: the social court route

If you have an employment contract with the administration, your tools are those of the Workers' Statute (Estatuto de los Trabajadores):

- A claim for protection of fundamental rights (tutela de derechos fundamentales) in the Social Court.
- Termination of the contract due to the employer's serious breach (Article 50).
- Compensation for moral and material damages.

We explain this in [what you can claim](/en/workplace-harassment/what-you-can-claim-for-harassment).

## Harassment in education

Schools and universities have their own dynamics: management teams, the education inspectorate (inspección educativa), staff meetings (claustros), permanent posts or temporary secondments (comisión de servicios). Harassment can come from management, from colleagues or even from families, and the school and the administration have a duty to protect their staff.

### Two cases from this firm

- **Castilla y León, 2011.** On 2 November 2011, the Administrative Chamber of the High Court of Justice of Castilla y León in Valladolid (Section 3, judgment 2491/2011, appeal 575/2007) declared the Regional Department of Education (Consejería de Educación) financially liable for the harassment suffered by a workshop teacher at the León School of Art (Escuela de Arte de León). The firm describes it as the first conviction of the education administration of Castilla y León for harassment.
- **Universidad Rey Juan Carlos, 2024.** As reported by El Español on 2 April 2024, the university was ordered to pay 122,963 euros to a full professor (catedrática) for failing to prevent the harassment she suffered and failing to apply psychosocial risk prevention.

Each case depends on its facts and its evidence. These results do not guarantee the outcome of another case, but they show that the administration can be held liable for failing to protect its staff.

### If you are a teacher

- Report the facts in writing to the management and, if the management is part of the problem, to the education inspectorate or the provincial or territorial directorate.
- Ask for the protocol of your regional department or university to be activated.
- Contact the occupational risk prevention service (servicio de prevención de riesgos laborales) and ask for a psychosocial risk assessment.
- If your health suffers, see your doctor and explain where it comes from. More in [sick leave for anxiety due to harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/sick-leave-for-anxiety-due-to-harassment).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an interim civil servant claim for harassment just like a career civil servant?

Yes. They have the same protection against harassment and the same ways to claim.

### Can I claim against both the administration and the harasser?

You can claim against the administration, which is liable for its employees. If the harassment is serious, it may also be a crime and the person can be reported. See [workplace harassment as a crime](/en/workplace-harassment/workplace-harassment-as-a-crime).

### How long do I have for a financial liability claim?

One year. If there is harm to your health, from when you recover or the lasting effects are established.

### Can they open disciplinary proceedings against me for reporting harassment?

They cannot retaliate against you for reporting. If they open proceedings against you as punishment, that itself can form part of the harassment.
