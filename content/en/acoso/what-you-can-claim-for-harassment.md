If you suffer workplace harassment (acoso laboral), you can ask the court to declare it, to make it stop and to award you compensation for the harm, including moral damages (daños morales). If you can no longer carry on, you can ask for your contract to be terminated with the compensation for an unfair dismissal (despido improcedente), plus damages. And you can claim not only against the company, but also against the person harassing you.

## The main routes

They are not mutually exclusive. They are often combined in the same claim. Which one suits you depends on what you want to achieve: to stay in your job in peace, or to leave the company with compensation.

## 1. Protection of fundamental rights

This is a special procedure before the Social Court (Juzgado de lo Social) for cases where rights such as your dignity, your moral integrity or your equality are violated (tutela de derechos fundamentales). It has priority and is faster than an ordinary trial.

With this claim you can ask for:

- A declaration that you have suffered harassment.
- The conduct to stop immediately.
- The situation to be put right: your duties, your post and your working hours given back to you.
- Compensation for the harm, both material and moral.

The Public Prosecutor (Ministerio Fiscal) takes part in these proceedings, and the burden-of-proof rule that favours you applies: if you provide serious indications, the company must justify its conduct. We explain how to gather them in [how to prove harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/how-to-prove-workplace-harassment).

This is the right route if you want to keep working, but without harassment.

## 2. Termination of the contract for serious breach (article 50 of the Workers' Statute)

If the situation is unbearable, do not resign. Article 50 of the Workers' Statute (Estatuto de los Trabajadores) allows you to ask the judge to terminate your contract because the company has seriously breached its obligations (extinción del contrato por incumplimiento grave). Harassment, which violates your dignity, fits those grounds.

If the judge rules in your favour:

- You receive the same compensation as in an unfair dismissal: 33 days' salary per year worked, up to a maximum of 24 months' pay. Time worked before 12 February 2012 is calculated at 45 days per year.
- You can receive unemployment benefit (paro), because it is an involuntary termination.
- You can add compensation for moral damages, for the violation of your fundamental rights.

You can calculate the statutory part of the compensation with our [compensation calculator](/calculadora-indemnizacion) (in Spanish).

> While the case is going through, you generally keep working. There are exceptions when continuing puts your health or your dignity at risk. We explain them in [quitting your job because of harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/quitting-your-job-because-of-harassment).

## 3. Compensation for moral damages

Moral damage is suffering, distress, harm to your reputation or to your life plans. It does not show up on a payslip, but it is compensated.

### How it is usually assessed

There is no fixed legal scale for harassment. The judge sets the amount according to the circumstances. The factors that usually weigh are:

- How serious the harassment was and how long it lasted.
- The harm to your health and whether it has left lasting effects (secuelas).
- The time spent on sick leave.
- The position of the harasser and whether the company tolerated or caused it.
- The deterrent effect: the compensation should also help to stop it happening again.

Because moral damage is hard to measure, the courts sometimes accept as a reference the fines in the Law on Infringements and Sanctions in the Social Order (Ley sobre Infracciones y Sanciones en el Orden Social, LISOS) for very serious infringements, which include harassment. Using it is not compulsory and it is not the only way to quantify the damage, but it gives the judge an objective point of reference.

Medical reports and, where appropriate, a psychological expert report (informe pericial psicológico) help to justify the amount you are claiming. Every case is different, so be wary of anyone who gives you a figure without knowing yours.

### Material damages

As well as moral damage, you can claim expenses and losses you can prove: treatment, a psychologist, the difference between your salary and what you receive on sick leave, among others.

## 4. Liability of the person harassing you

It is not only the company that is liable. In the proceedings for protection of fundamental rights you can also sue the person harassing you, and the judge can order both to pay jointly and severally (de forma solidaria). That means you can claim the full amount from either of them.

In 2005, at Social Court No. 29 of Madrid (Juzgado de lo Social n.º 29 de Madrid), this firm obtained a judgment ordering a company and the harassing manager, jointly and severally, to pay 30,000 euros to the worker. The High Court of Justice of Madrid (Tribunal Superior de Justicia de Madrid) upheld the judgment.

If the harassment is especially serious, it may also be a crime. We explain it in [workplace harassment as a crime](/en/workplace-harassment/workplace-harassment-as-a-crime).

## 5. Other complementary routes

- **A complaint to the Labour Inspectorate (Inspección de Trabajo).** It can sanction the company and its report serves as evidence.
- **Benefits surcharge (recargo de prestaciones).** If your sick leave is recognised as a work accident (accidente de trabajo) and the company did not comply with its prevention duties, you can ask for a surcharge on your benefits, paid by the company. It depends on the case.
- **Determination of the cause of the sick leave (determinación de contingencia).** So that your sick leave counts as a work accident. See [sick leave for anxiety due to harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/sick-leave-for-anxiety-due-to-harassment).

## Deadlines

Deadlines are what ruin the most cases. These are the general ones:

- **Claims for amounts owed and damages:** 1 year (article 59 of the Workers' Statute). With harassment, which is continuing conduct, the deadline usually starts to run when it stops.
- **Termination of the contract under article 50:** it has to be requested while the contract is still in force. If you are dismissed first, the strategy changes.
- **If you are dismissed:** 20 working days (días hábiles) to challenge the dismissal. August counts.

> For most claims you must first file a request for conciliation (papeleta de conciliación); for the protection of fundamental rights it is not compulsory. Check your dates with our [deadlines](/herramientas/plazos) (in Spanish) tool.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I claim moral damages without leaving the company?

Yes. A claim for protection of fundamental rights allows you to ask for compensation and keep working.

### How much can I get for moral damages?

It depends on how serious the harassment was, how long it lasted and the harm to your health. There is no fixed figure and we cannot give you one without studying your case.

### Can I sue my boss personally?

Yes. In the proceedings for protection of fundamental rights, both the company and the person harassing you can be sued, and both can be ordered to pay.

### Do I get unemployment benefit if my contract is terminated under article 50?

Yes, if the judge declares the termination. It is treated as an involuntary termination.
