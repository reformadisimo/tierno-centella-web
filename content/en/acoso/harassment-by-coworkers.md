Horizontal harassment (acoso horizontal) is harassment by coworkers at your own level, who have no authority over you. It is just as much harassment as when it comes from a boss. And the company cannot wash its hands of it: it has a duty to protect your health and your dignity, and if it knows what is happening and does not act, it is liable for it.

## What horizontal harassment is

It is harassment that comes from the people who work alongside you: one coworker or, often, a group. There is no order from above, but the effect is just as harmful.

The most common forms are:

- Freezing you out: not speaking to you, not inviting you, changing the subject when you arrive.
- Mockery, nicknames, repeated cruel jokes.
- Rumours about your work or your private life.
- Hiding information you need to do your job, or sabotaging your tasks.
- Blaming you for other people's mistakes or taking credit for your achievements.
- Disparaging comments about your age, origin, appearance, sexual orientation, disability or any other personal circumstance.

As in all other cases, what makes it harassment is repetition and harm. A one-off argument is not harassment. If you are unsure, read [how to know if you are being harassed](/en/workplace-harassment/am-i-being-harassed-at-work).

## Why the company is liable

The Occupational Risk Prevention Act (Ley de Prevención de Riesgos Laborales) requires the company to protect the safety and health of its staff, and that includes psychosocial risks. The Workers' Statute (Estatuto de los Trabajadores) recognises your right to have your dignity respected at work.

That means the company:

- Must prevent harassment by assessing psychosocial risks.
- Must have channels for you to report it and, for sexual and sex-based harassment, a specific protocol.
- Must investigate when it becomes aware of it.
- Must take measures to stop it: separating the people involved, changing shifts, disciplining the harasser.

> If the company knows about the harassment and does nothing, its inaction is in itself a breach. It is no longer just a problem between coworkers: it is the company's problem.

That is why it is so important that the company knows, and that you can prove it knew.

## How to report it

### 1. Prepare the facts

Before you write, put what happened in order: dates, what happened, who took part, who saw it. A diary of events will help you. We explain how to keep one in [how to prove harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/how-to-prove-workplace-harassment).

### 2. Report it in writing

A spoken complaint in the corridor gets forgotten or denied. Put it in writing:

- An email to your manager and to human resources, keeping a copy.
- The company's whistleblowing channel (canal de denuncias) or harassment protocol, if there is one.
- If there is no reply, a burofax (a certified letter with proof of content and delivery).

### 3. What to put in the letter

- That you want to report a situation of harassment.
- The specific facts, with dates and names, without insults or adjectives.
- How it is affecting you, including your health if that is the case.
- That you are asking for the protocol to be activated and for measures to be taken to stop it.

You do not need to give it a legal label. Describing what is happening is enough. If you can, have a lawyer review it before you send it.

### 4. Ask for the protocol to be activated

If the company has a protocol, insist that it is applied. If it does not have one, that in itself is a relevant fact. We explain it in [anti-harassment protocol](/en/workplace-harassment/anti-harassment-protocol).

### 5. If the company does not act

- File a complaint with the Labour Inspectorate (Inspección de Trabajo). It is free of charge and the Inspectorate does not reveal to the company who made the complaint.
- Talk to the workers' representatives or the health and safety representative (delegado de prevención), if there are any.
- Assess with a lawyer a claim against the company and, where appropriate, against the coworkers who are harassing you.

## What you should avoid

- Responding in kind. It could get you disciplined and weaken your position.
- Confronting the group in public.
- Stopping going to work without medical sick leave (baja médica).
- Resigning. You would lose your compensation and your unemployment benefit (paro). There are other ways out, which we explain in [quitting your job because of harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/quitting-your-job-because-of-harassment).

## What you can claim

With horizontal harassment, the routes are the same as in all other cases:

- That the company takes measures to stop it.
- Compensation for damages, against the company for failing to protect you and also, where appropriate, against the coworkers who harass you.
- Termination of your contract with compensation if the situation is unbearable and the company does not react.

We go into detail in [what you can claim](/en/workplace-harassment/what-you-can-claim-for-harassment).

> The deadline to claim damages is one year. Check your dates in [deadlines](/en/tools/deadlines).

If your health suffers, see your doctor and explain that the cause is work-related. We explain how in [sick leave for anxiety due to harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/sick-leave-for-anxiety-due-to-harassment).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the company liable even if the harasser is not my boss?

Yes, if it knew or should have known what was happening and did not act. Its duty to protect you does not depend on who the harasser is.

### Can they discipline me for complaining?

No. Retaliation for complaining about harassment can be declared void (nula). If they dismiss you for it, read [quitting your job because of harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/quitting-your-job-because-of-harassment).

### What if my coworkers say it was just jokes?

What counts is the repetition, the effect on you and whether it was humiliating. A repeated joke that degrades you does not stop being harassment just because someone calls it a joke.

### Do I have to tell my direct boss first?

Not necessarily. If your boss is part of the problem or tolerates it, go to human resources or to the whistleblowing channel.
