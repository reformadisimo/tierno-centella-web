The anti-harassment protocol (protocolo de acoso) is the internal procedure that sets out how the company prevents, receives and investigates harassment complaints and what measures it takes. The protocol against sexual and sex-based harassment (acoso sexual y por razón de sexo) is compulsory in all companies, whatever their size. As for psychological or workplace harassment (acoso moral), the company is obliged to prevent it as part of its psychosocial risk assessment (evaluación de riesgos psicosociales), and many collective agreements (convenios colectivos) also require a protocol.

## What the anti-harassment protocol is

It is a document that sets out, step by step, what the company does when someone reports a situation of harassment: who to contact, who investigates, within what time limits, what guarantees each party has and what measures can be taken.

Its purpose is to make sure the problem does not depend on anyone's goodwill. And it helps you: if the company has a protocol and does not apply it, or applies it badly, that will be evidence of its breach.

## When it is compulsory

### Sexual and sex-based harassment

Organic Law 3/2007 on equality (Ley Orgánica 3/2007, de igualdad) obliges all companies to promote conditions that prevent this harassment and to establish procedures to prevent it and to handle complaints. In practice this means a protocol, compulsory in all companies, including small ones.

### Harassment against LGBTI people

Since Law 4/2023 (Ley 4/2023) and Royal Decree 1026/2024 (Real Decreto 1026/2024), companies with more than 50 workers must have a protocol for dealing with harassment and violence against LGBTI people, as part of a set of planned measures.

### Psychological or workplace harassment

There is no law that requires all companies, as a general rule, to have a protocol against psychological harassment. But the Occupational Risk Prevention Law (Ley de Prevención de Riesgos Laborales) obliges companies to assess and prevent psychosocial risks, and harassment is one of them. In addition, many collective agreements and equality plans (planes de igualdad) do require a protocol against any kind of harassment. Check yours.

> Even if your company has no protocol against psychological harassment, it is still obliged to protect you. Not having a protocol does not free it from liability.

### ILO Convention 190

Convention 190 of the International Labour Organization (Organización Internacional del Trabajo, OIT) on violence and harassment has been in force in Spain since 25 May 2023. It recognises everyone's right to a world of work free from violence and harassment, including psychological harassment, and asks States to require companies to adopt policies to prevent it, to take these risks into account in managing safety and health, and to provide ways to complain and to investigate. The courts use it to interpret companies' obligations.

## What a good protocol should include

There is no single model, but a serious protocol usually includes:

- **A clear statement** that the company does not tolerate harassment.
- **Definitions and examples** of what is considered harassment.
- **A channel for reporting it**, with alternatives if the person in charge is part of the problem.
- **Who investigates**: a person or committee with training and independence.
- **Short time limits** for each stage.
- **Guarantees**: confidentiality, respect, impartiality and a ban on retaliation against anyone who reports or gives evidence.
- **Interim measures (medidas cautelares)** during the investigation, such as separating the people involved.
- **How it is closed**: with a written conclusion and, if harassment is confirmed, with measures and sanctions.
- **Support for the victim** and follow-up afterwards.

You have the right to know what the protocol says. Ask human resources or the workers' representatives (representación de los trabajadores) for it.

## How to activate it

1. **Get the protocol** and read which channel and format it requires.
2. **Report the facts in writing** through that channel, with dates, names and witnesses, without insults or opinions. Keep proof that you sent it.
3. **Expressly ask** for the protocol to be activated and for interim measures to be taken if you are still coming into contact with the person harassing you.
4. **Cooperate** with the investigation, provide your evidence and suggest witnesses.
5. **Ask in writing** for the conclusion of the procedure.

Before you take the step, gather your evidence. We explain how in [how to prove harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/how-to-prove-workplace-harassment).

> Activating the protocol does not stop the deadlines for going to court. Do not wait for it to finish if a deadline is about to expire. Check [deadlines](/en/tools/deadlines).

## What happens if the company does not have one or does not apply it

The company may:

- Not have a protocol when it is obliged to have one.
- Have one, but not activate it when you ask.
- Activate it, but superficially, without hearing the witnesses or with an investigator who is not impartial.
- Conclude that there is no harassment without really investigating.
- Retaliate against you for activating it.

In all these cases you have options:

- **Report it to the Labour Inspectorate (Inspección de Trabajo)**, which can check whether the protocol exists and whether it is applied, review the psychosocial risk assessment and propose sanctions.
- **Use it as evidence** in your claim: the company's failure to act strengthens your case.
- **Go to court** to claim protection of your rights (tutela de derechos), compensation or termination of your contract. We explain it in [what you can claim](/en/workplace-harassment/what-you-can-claim-for-harassment).

If you are retaliated against for activating it, that may be void (nulo). More in [quitting your job because of harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/quitting-your-job-because-of-harassment).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does my small company have to have a protocol?

For sexual and sex-based harassment, yes, whatever its size. For psychological harassment it depends on your collective agreement, but the obligation to prevent it always exists.

### Do I have to go through the protocol before going to court?

It is not compulsory, but it is highly recommended. It records that the company knew.

### Who finds out what I report?

The protocol must guarantee confidentiality. Only those investigating and the people involved, to the extent necessary, should know about it.

### What if the protocol concludes there is no harassment?

That is not final. A judge may assess the facts differently, and a badly conducted investigation can turn against the company.
