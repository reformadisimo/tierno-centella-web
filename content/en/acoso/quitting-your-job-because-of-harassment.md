If harassment is pushing you to leave, do not resign (baja voluntaria): you would lose your compensation and your unemployment benefit (paro). The right way out is to ask the court to terminate your contract because of the company's serious breach of its obligations (extinción del contrato por incumplimiento grave, article 50 of the Workers' Statute, Estatuto de los Trabajadores). If the court rules in your favour, you receive the compensation for an unfair dismissal (despido improcedente), you can add moral damages (daños morales) and you are entitled to unemployment benefit.

## Do not resign

It is the most understandable reaction and the one that does the most harm. When work becomes unbearable, leaving seems like the only way out. But a resignation has consequences:

- **You get no compensation.** You are leaving of your own free will.
- **You are not entitled to unemployment benefit.** Unemployment benefit requires an involuntary loss of your job.
- **You weaken your claim.** The company will be able to say you left because you wanted to.

> Very often the aim of the harassment is precisely to make you leave on your own. If you resign, the harasser gets what they wanted at no cost.

If you are at your limit, the first step is to look after your health. If you need to stop, the way to do it is medical sick leave (baja médica), not a resignation. We explain it in [sick leave for anxiety due to harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/sick-leave-for-anxiety-due-to-harassment).

## Termination of the contract for serious breach (article 50)

Article 50 of the Workers' Statute allows you to ask for your contract to be terminated when the company seriously breaches its obligations. The grounds include any serious breach, and the law expressly mentions changes to working conditions that undermine your dignity. Harassment, which attacks your dignity and your moral integrity, fits.

### What you get

If the court upholds your claim:

- **Compensation as in an unfair dismissal.** 33 days' salary per year worked, up to a maximum of 24 months' pay. Time worked before 12 February 2012 is calculated at 45 days per year. You can estimate it with the [compensation calculator](/en/tools/compensation-calculator).
- **Moral damages.** If the violation of your fundamental rights is proven, you can add further compensation. See [what you can claim](/en/workplace-harassment/what-you-can-claim-for-harassment).
- **Unemployment benefit.** Termination through this route is considered involuntary, so you can receive unemployment benefit (prestación por desempleo) if you meet the contribution requirements.

### You ask the court; you do not decide it alone

The termination does not happen because you decide it or tell the company. It is declared by a judge. The usual process is:

1. File the request for conciliation (papeleta de conciliación).
2. If there is no agreement, file the claim with the Social Court (Juzgado de lo Social).
3. Wait for the trial and the judgment.

There may be an agreement before that, at the conciliation stage or on the day of the trial itself.

### In general, you keep working in the meantime

This is the hardest part. As a rule, while the claim is going through, the contract remains in force and you must keep going to work. If you stop going without justification, the company could dismiss you for absences and your claim would lose its basis.

There are exceptions:

- **If you are on medical sick leave**, you do not have to go in. Sick leave does not prevent you from filing the claim.
- **Interim measures (medidas cautelares).** When the company's conduct harms your dignity or your physical or moral integrity, procedural law (article 79.7 of the Law Regulating the Social Jurisdiction, Ley Reguladora de la Jurisdicción Social) allows you to ask the judge to release you from working while the proceedings last, with the company still obliged to pay your salary and social security contributions. You have to request it and justify it; the judge decides.
- **Exceptional cases** in which continuing to work puts your health or your dignity at serious risk. The courts have accepted this in some cases, but it is risky to do it without the backing of a court.

> Do not stop going to work on your own initiative without talking to a lawyer first. It is one of the mistakes that sinks the most claims.

## If you are dismissed after reporting the harassment

It happens often: you complain about the harassment, activate the protocol or go to the Labour Inspectorate (Inspección de Trabajo), and shortly afterwards you are dismissed. The law protects you against this with what is known as the guarantee against retaliation (garantía de indemnidad): the company cannot punish you for claiming your rights.

If the dismissal is retaliation, it can be declared void (despido nulo). A void dismissal obliges the company to:

- Reinstate you in your job, on the same terms.
- Pay you the wages you did not receive from the date of dismissal.
- Compensate you for the damage it has caused you.

An example not handled by this firm, discussed as case law: Social Court No. 1 of Córdoba (Juzgado de lo Social n.º 1 de Córdoba) declared void the dismissal of a supermarket worker who had reported harassment, because it violated the guarantee against retaliation.

It is not enough for the dismissal to come after your complaint. You have to provide indications that the two are connected: how close together they are in time, that there were no problems before, that the reasons for the dismissal are weak. If you provide them, the company will have to prove that the dismissal had nothing to do with it.

> You have 20 working days (días hábiles) to challenge a dismissal, and August counts. If you are dismissed, do not wait. Check your dates in [deadlines](/en/tools/deadlines).

Since Supreme Court (Tribunal Supremo) judgment 1250/2024 of 18 November, for disciplinary dismissals after that date the company must first give you the opportunity to defend yourself against the accusations (prior hearing, audiencia previa). The Supreme Court has since held that, if it does not, the dismissal is unfair. More information in [dismissal](/en/dismissal).

## How to prepare before taking the step

- Gather your evidence: a diary of events, messages, witnesses, medical reports. See [how to prove harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/how-to-prove-workplace-harassment).
- Report the harassment to the company in writing, so it cannot say it did not know.
- Check your payslip and your length of service, to calculate the compensation.
- Talk to a lawyer before making a move. The order of the steps matters.

## Frequently asked questions

### If I resign because of harassment, can I claim afterwards?

It is much more difficult. You lose the article 50 compensation and unemployment benefit. You might be able to claim some damages, but your position is much weaker.

### Can I ask for termination while on sick leave?

Yes. Sick leave does not prevent you from filing the claim and, while it lasts, you do not have to go to work.

### How long does the trial take?

It depends on the court. It can take anywhere from a few months to more than a year. That is why interim measures are important when continuing to work is harming you.

### What if the company dismisses me while my claim is under way?

You will need to challenge the dismissal within 20 working days. If it is retaliation for your claim, it may be void.
