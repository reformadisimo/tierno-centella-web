Workplace harassment has been a crime in Spain since 2010, when it was included in Article 173.1 of the Criminal Code (Código Penal). It is punishable by six months to two years in prison for anyone who, taking advantage of their position of superiority at work, repeatedly carries out hostile or humiliating acts that amount to serious harassment. The criminal route can be used in addition to the employment route, but it is reserved for the most serious cases.

## What the law says

Organic Law 5/2010 (Ley Orgánica 5/2010) added a specific paragraph on harassment at work to Article 173.1 of the Criminal Code. It punishes those who, within any employment or civil service relationship and taking advantage of their position of superiority, repeatedly carry out hostile or humiliating acts against another person which, without amounting to degrading treatment (trato degradante), constitute serious harassment of the victim.

It applies both in private companies and in the public administration.

## Requirements for it to be a crime

All of them must be met.

### 1. An employment or civil service relationship

The harassment has to happen at work: between employees of a company or between public employees.

### 2. A relationship of superiority

The harasser has to take advantage of their position above the victim: a boss, a director, a mayor in relation to a town council employee. That is why this crime fits top-down harassment (acoso descendente).

> Harassment between coworkers at the same level does not, in principle, fall under this paragraph of Article 173.1. It may be a different crime if there are threats, coercion or physical or psychological injuries (lesiones), and it can always be claimed through the employment route.

### 3. Repeated hostile or humiliating acts

One episode is not enough. There has to be repetition: insults, contempt, isolation, degrading orders, mockery.

### 4. That they amount to serious harassment

This is the most demanding requirement. Not all workplace harassment is a crime. The criminal courts reserve this route for especially serious situations, because of the intensity of the conduct, its duration or the harm caused. A case that is won in the Social Court (Juzgado de lo Social) may not reach this level.

## The penalties

- **Six months to two years in prison** for the crime of workplace harassment.
- If the harassment harms your health, for example with an anxiety disorder or depression, the injuries can be punished separately.
- The convicted person must also compensate the victim for the harm caused, which is known as civil liability (responsabilidad civil).
- If the harasser is a public authority or civil servant, there may be penalties of disqualification from office (inhabilitación).

## An example from the courts

This is a case not handled by this firm, which we discuss as case law. Criminal Court No. 1 of Don Benito (Badajoz) (Juzgado de lo Penal n.º 1 de Don Benito) sentenced the mayor of Castuera to three years in prison for workplace harassment and injuries to the municipal architect, and ordered him to pay 33,100 euros in compensation for the harm to his mental health. According to the judgment, over several periods of years he carried out humiliating and degrading acts to make the employee leave his post, which caused him an anxiety-depressive syndrome. The judgment was not final when it was published and could be appealed.

The case illustrates the requirements: superiority (a mayor over an employee), repetition over years and serious, proven harm.

## How to report it

You can file a criminal complaint (denuncia) at a police station, at a Guardia Civil station or directly at the duty court (juzgado de guardia). You can also file a private criminal complaint (querella), a more technical document with a lawyer and a court representative (procurador), which allows you to act as a private prosecutor (acusación particular) from the start.

For the complaint to succeed, you need evidence: a diary of events, messages, witnesses, medical reports and, often, an expert psychological report. We explain how to gather it in [how to prove harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/how-to-prove-workplace-harassment).

> A criminal complaint without sufficient evidence usually ends up being dismissed (archivada). Before reporting, it is worth assessing with a lawyer whether the case has the seriousness and the evidence that the criminal route requires.

## Limitation period

The crime of workplace harassment becomes time-barred (prescribe) after five years, because its maximum penalty is under five years in prison. In a continuing offence like this one, the period generally runs from the last act of harassment.

Be careful: the deadlines in the employment route are much shorter. The fact that the crime is not yet time-barred does not mean you are still in time to claim in the Social Court. See [deadlines](/en/tools/deadlines).

## Combining it with the employment route

You can use both, but they have to be coordinated:

- **The employment route** is directed mainly against the company, which is liable for failing to protect you. It allows you to claim damages, terminate the contract or have the harassment stopped. See [what you can claim](/en/workplace-harassment/what-you-can-claim-for-harassment).
- **The criminal route** is directed against the person who harasses you. It seeks their punishment and compensation.

What you cannot do is get paid twice for the same harm. If you are compensated in one route, that will be taken into account in the other.

In many cases you start with the employment route, which is faster and requires less seriousness, and keep the criminal route for the most serious situations. The strategy depends on your case.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is all workplace harassment a crime?

No. Only harassment that is serious, repeated and carried out from a position of superiority. Most cases are resolved through the employment route.

### Can I report a coworker for workplace harassment?

This crime requires superiority, so in principle no. But if there are threats, coercion or injuries, it may be a different crime. And the company is liable through the employment route.

### How long do I have to report it?

The crime becomes time-barred after five years, generally counted from the last act. The employment deadlines are shorter.

### Do I need a lawyer to report it?

For a criminal complaint (denuncia), no. For a private criminal complaint (querella) or to join the proceedings as a private prosecutor, yes.
