Workplace harassment is proved by adding up pieces of evidence, not with a single one. What works best is a diary of events with dates, backed up by emails, messages, witnesses and medical reports that show the harm to your health. If at trial you provide solid indications (indicios) that your fundamental rights have been violated, it is the company that has to prove that its conduct had a reasonable justification.

## Why evidence is the most important thing

Harassment usually happens without witnesses, or with witnesses who are afraid to speak. That is why many strong cases are lost for lack of evidence, not because the worker was in the wrong.

The good news is that you do not have to prove everything one hundred per cent. In proceedings for violation of fundamental rights, the law shares the burden of proof (carga de la prueba): you provide serious indications and the company must show that what it did had nothing to do with harassing you.

> Start gathering evidence today, even if you have not yet decided to make a claim. Whatever you do not keep now may disappear.

To see at a glance what evidence you have and what you are missing, use our [evidence map](/herramientas/mapa-de-pruebas) (in Spanish).

## 1. The diary of events

This is the foundation of everything. A notebook or a document where you write down each episode, as soon as possible after it happens.

In each entry, include:

- Date and time.
- Place.
- What happened and what was said, with the exact words if you remember them.
- Who did it and who was present.
- How you felt and whether it had any effect on your health.
- Whether there is any evidence to back it up (an email, a message).

The diary on its own does not prove the harassment, because you are the one who writes it. But it puts the facts in order, helps you remember at trial and shows your lawyer what to look for. Keep it off the company's devices.

## 2. Emails, WhatsApp and other messages

Written messages are among the most useful evidence. They show the tone, the frequency and who says what.

- **Emails.** Forward to your personal email only what directly concerns you. Avoid taking confidential company information that has nothing to do with your case, because it could be turned against you.
- **WhatsApp and chats.** Do not delete conversations. Take screenshots, but also keep the phone and the original conversation. If the other side denies the messages, a notarial verification (cotejo notarial) or a digital forensics expert (perito informático) may be needed.
- **Instructions and changes of duties.** Keep any document showing that you are being stripped of tasks, moved to another shift or given work below your job category.

## 3. Recordings

Recording a conversation that you take part in is legal in Spain. This is what the Constitutional Court (Tribunal Constitucional) has held: there is no breach of the secrecy of communications when the person recording is one of the participants.

What you cannot do is record conversations you are not part of, for example by leaving your phone recording in a room when you leave. That may be illegal and the evidence could be thrown out.

> A legal recording is not permission to spread it. Use it only to defend yourself in your claim and do not share it on social media or with third parties.

Keep the original file unedited and note the date and context.

## 4. Witnesses

Coworkers, former coworkers, customers or suppliers who have seen what happened. A coworker who is still at the company may be afraid; a former coworker usually speaks more freely.

- Make a list of who saw each episode.
- Do not pressure anyone. A witness who is pushed into it is not very convincing.
- Bear in mind that anyone who gives evidence as a witness is protected against retaliation for doing so.

## 5. Medical and psychological reports

Harassment leaves its mark on your health. That mark is evidence.

- Go to your GP (médico de cabecera) and explain that your distress comes from work. Ask for this to be recorded in your medical history.
- If you are referred to mental health services, or you see a private psychologist, keep the reports.
- If you are put on sick leave, keep all the sick notes (partes). We explain how to have it recognised as a work accident in [sick leave for anxiety due to harassment](/en/workplace-harassment/sick-leave-for-anxiety-due-to-harassment).

A medical report does not prove who is harassing you, but it does prove that your health has been harmed and when it started. Combined with everything else, it carries a lot of weight.

## 6. Written notice to the company

Tell the company what is happening, in writing and in a way that records the date: an email to human resources, a burofax (a certified letter with proof of content and delivery) or hand delivery with a stamped copy.

This does two things. First, the company can no longer say it did not know. Second, it should activate its harassment protocol. If it does nothing, that inaction is one more piece of evidence. We explain it in [anti-harassment protocol](/en/workplace-harassment/anti-harassment-protocol).

Describe specific facts, without insults or opinions. Before sending it, it is a good idea to have a lawyer review it.

## 7. Labour Inspectorate

You can file a complaint with the Labour and Social Security Inspectorate (Inspección de Trabajo y Seguridad Social). It is free of charge. The Inspectorate keeps the identity of the person who complained secret from the company.

The Inspectorate can visit the company, review the psychosocial risk assessment, ask for the protocol and talk to the staff. Its report, if it confirms the facts, is very valuable evidence at trial.

## 8. Expert evidence

An expert psychologist or psychiatrist (perito) can assess your condition, its link to your work and how serious the harm is. This is especially useful for claiming non-material damages (daños morales). It is not always necessary; it depends on the case and on what the medical reports already show.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Waiting months without writing anything down.
- Deleting messages or changing phones without making a backup.
- Taking confidential company documents.
- Recording conversations you are not part of.
- Confronting the harasser in public. It can be used against you.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I record my boss without telling them?

Yes, if you take part in the conversation. You do not need to warn them. What is not legal is recording other people's conversations.

### Are WhatsApp screenshots valid?

Yes, but if the other side denies them, it is advisable to have the original phone and, sometimes, a verification or a digital forensics expert.

### What if I have no witnesses?

You can win without witnesses if there are other indications: messages, medical reports, documented changes of duties, the company's failure to react.

### How far back can I go?

Everything you can document helps to show the pattern. The deadlines for making a claim are a separate matter; check them in [deadlines](/herramientas/plazos) (in Spanish).
