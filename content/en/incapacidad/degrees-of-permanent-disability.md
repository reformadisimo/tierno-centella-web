Permanent disability (incapacidad permanente) has four degrees: partial, total, absolute and severe disability (gran incapacidad). Each one is recognised according to how much your injuries limit your ability to work, and each pays differently: partial disability is a one-off payment of 24 months' benefit; total disability, 55 % of your benefit base (base reguladora) (75 % if you are 55 or over and meet the requirements); absolute disability, 100 %; and severe disability, the corresponding pension plus a supplement to pay whoever helps you.

> If the INSS (Spanish Social Security) refuses your disability or recognises a lower degree than you believe you are entitled to, you have 30 working days to file the prior administrative claim (reclamación previa). We explain this in [the guide on the prior claim and lawsuit](/en/permanent-disability/appealing-a-denied-permanent-disability).

## What permanent disability is

It is the situation of a person who, after medical treatment, has serious injuries that are expected to be permanent or long-lasting and that reduce or eliminate their ability to work. What is assessed is not the illness itself, but how it affects your work.

It is declared by the INSS (Instituto Nacional de la Seguridad Social), after the Disability Assessment Team (Equipo de Valoración de Incapacidades, EVI), which many people call the medical tribunal (tribunal médico), issues its report (dictamen). The origin of the disability also matters: it is not calculated in the same way if it comes from a common illness (enfermedad común) as from an accident at work (accidente de trabajo) or an occupational disease (enfermedad profesional).

## The four degrees

### Partial permanent disability

This recognises a reduction of at least 33 % in your normal performance in your usual occupation, but you can still carry out its fundamental tasks.

- What you get: a one-off lump-sum payment (a tanto alzado) of 24 months of the benefit base used to calculate your temporary disability (incapacidad temporal).
- You can keep working in the same job.

### Total permanent disability

You cannot carry out all of the tasks of your usual occupation, or its fundamental tasks, but you could work in a different one.

- What you get: a lifelong pension of 55 % of your benefit base.
- Qualified total disability (total cualificada): if you are 55 or over and, because of your age, your training or where you live, it is difficult for you to find another job, the pension can go up by 20 points, to 75 %. You have to apply for it, and that increase is not paid while you are working.
- If you are under 60, in exceptional cases it can be replaced by a one-off payment.
- It is compatible with working in a different occupation.

### Absolute permanent disability

You cannot carry out any profession or trade on a regular basis.

- What you get: a lifelong pension of 100 % of your benefit base.
- The law does not entirely prohibit all activity, but compatibility with a job has limits and it is worth checking beforehand.

### Severe disability

As well as having a permanent disability, you need another person's help for basic everyday activities, such as getting dressed, eating, washing or getting around. It used to be called gran invalidez; Law 2/2025 (Ley 2/2025) changed the name.

- What you get: the pension for the corresponding degree of disability (normally absolute) plus a supplement.
- The supplement is the sum of 45 % of the minimum contribution base in force when the disability arises and 30 % of your last contribution base for the contingency it stems from.
- That supplement cannot be less than 45 % of the pension you receive without it.

> The severe disability supplement is designed so you can pay the person who looks after you. It is added to the pension and is not a fixed percentage: it depends on your contribution bases.

## How the benefit base is calculated

The benefit base is the figure to which the percentages are applied. How it is calculated depends on the origin:

- Common illness: the contribution bases from the last few years are used (with rules for periods without contributions), and a percentage is also applied according to the years you have contributed. That is why two people with the same degree can receive very different amounts.
- Non-work accident: it is calculated using the bases from a contribution period chosen according to the rules.
- Accident at work or occupational disease: your actual wages from the last year are used, including extra payments (pagas extra) and other items.

If your disability comes from an accident at work or an occupational disease, no minimum contribution period is required. If it comes from a common illness, it is, and it depends on your age. In both cases it is worth checking whether your sick leave has been correctly classified; we cover this, together with the determination of contingency (determinación de contingencia), on [the permanent disability page](/en/permanent-disability).

There are also minimum pensions set each year by the budget law or the revaluation decree, which may improve what you receive.

## What happens to your job

Until 2025, total disability, absolute disability or severe disability automatically ended your contract. Since Law 2/2025 of 29 April (Ley 2/2025, de 29 de abril), that is no longer the case: you can ask to stay with the company and it must try to adapt your job or move you to another one. We explain this in [the guide on Law 2/2025](/en/permanent-disability/permanent-disability-and-your-contract).

## Review of the degree

Permanent disability can be reviewed if you get worse, get better or there was a diagnostic error. The decision (resolución) itself states from when a review can be requested. If your situation has got worse, you can apply for a higher degree.

## What you should do

1. Gather all your medical reports, especially those from specialists, and make sure they explain specific limitations for your work.
2. Prepare a realistic description of the tasks in your job. The EVI assesses your usual occupation, not a generic one.
3. Read the report and the decision carefully, and write down the date you were notified.
4. If you do not agree, do not let the 30 working days for the prior administrative claim pass.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I work if I have total permanent disability?

Yes, in an occupation different from your usual one. In the same occupation, the pension is suspended while you work in that adapted job.

### What is the difference between permanent disability (incapacidad) and disability status (discapacidad)?

Permanent disability is recognised by the INSS and refers to your ability to work. The degree of disability status (grado de discapacidad) is recognised by the Autonomous Community (Comunidad Autónoma, the regional government) and measures your limitations in general. They are separate procedures, although the law establishes equivalences for some purposes.

### Is the disability pension subject to income tax (IRPF)?

Pensions for absolute permanent disability and severe disability are exempt from income tax (IRPF). Total and partial disability are generally taxed. It is worth checking this with your adviser.

### Is the 75 % for qualified total disability automatic at 55?

No. You have to apply for it, and your age and other circumstances must make it difficult to find a different job. Also, that increase stops being paid while you are working.
